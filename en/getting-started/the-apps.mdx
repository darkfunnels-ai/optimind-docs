DarkFunnels isn't a single screen: it's **pieces that switch on and off, one by
one, in your business**. Each piece is an **app**. If an app is on, it shows up
in the left-hand menu; if it's off, that row simply doesn't exist for you.

That's where the number-one confusion comes from: **two businesses open the same
dashboard and see different menus.** Neither one is broken. One has more apps
switched on than the other.

<Note>
**Why this page exists.** Other guides tell you things like *"you need the
Orders app active"*. If nobody has explained what an app is, that sentence makes
no sense. This is the explanation, and also the map of the whole menu.
</Note>

## The apps that exist today

There are **six business apps**. Four come switched on when you open the
account; the other two you switch on yourself if you want them.

| App | On by default? | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sales** | Yes | Your agents: the script, the conversations, the catalog, the files and the results. It's the heart of the product. |
| **Calendar** | Yes | The schedule: appointments, services, business hours and a booking page. |
| **Inventory** | Yes | Real stock, with ins and outs per warehouse. |
| **Orders** | Yes | The orders your agents close, with what was charged and what was delivered. |
| **Shipping** | No | Register the shipment for an order and track it. |
| **Electronic invoicing** | No | Issuer, series and electronic receipts. |

<Warning>
**Shipping and Electronic invoicing don't come in the standard package.** They
are off when you open the account and you have to install them yourself from
**Administration → Apps**. And before you do, read
**What's live and what's half-built** further down: the two are not equally
finished.
</Warning>

### And three pieces you don't touch

In **Administration → Apps** you'll see **nine** cards, not six. The three extra
ones are internal pieces tagged **Core**, with no buttons, because they can't be
switched off or uninstalled:

| Piece | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **DarkFunnels Copilot** | The AI that lives inside the dashboard and helps you build and fix your agent. |
| **Identity and access** | Who gets into your account and with what permissions. |
| **MCP bridge** | The optional connection for operating your business from Claude, ChatGPT or Codex. |

<Note>
**The Copilot has no menu entry** and you won't find one however hard you look:
it's the chat panel pinned to the right-hand side of the screen, and it goes
with you through every section. On narrow screens it opens as a drawer that
overlays the page. On the public screens — sign in, sign up, accept an
invitation — it isn't there.

**The MCP bridge** is Core, but that doesn't mean you already have it installed.
Its card looks the same whether it is installed or not, because Core cards show
the tag instead of the status.
[The connection URL](/en/reference/connection-url) covers it.
</Note>

## The sidebar, top to bottom

The order is set by hand and **is always the same**: it isn't alphabetical and
doesn't depend on which one you activated first. Apps that are off leave no
gap: they're skipped.

appear in Spanish.

    </Tip>

## App, agent and funnel are not the same thing

Three words a newcomer mixes up on day one:

| Word | What it is | Whose it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **App** | A piece of the dashboard, switched on or off. | **The whole business's**. |
| **Agent** | Your automatic salesperson: a name, a script and a WhatsApp number. | It lives **inside the Sales app**. |
| **Funnel** | Another name for the agent. **It's the same thing.** In the dashboard, "agent" is the word that rules. | Same as the agent. |

→ [Glossary](/en/reference/glossary)

The practical consequence: **switching agents in the selector doesn't change
what you see in Calendar, Inventory, Orders, Shipping or Electronic
invoicing.** Stock and orders belong to the business, not to a salesperson.
That's why the selector hangs off **Sales** and not off the menu's first level.
And that's also why switching agents doesn't take you out of the screen you're
on: you stay where you were.

<Note>
**Two things that confuse people here, and are worth saying:**

**1. The Calendar does distinguish agents.** Each agent has its own schedule —
it's just that you choose between them **inside the Calendar screen itself**,
with the selector you see at the top left, and there you can look at several
overlaid.

**2. The Orders screen uses the word "funnel" in its subtitle**
(*"Everything the funnel achieves — orders, appointments, services and leads —
from the 'yes' to the close"*). It's a turn of phrase: the order list you see is
your whole business's.
</Note>

## What's live and what's half-built

I'd rather say it before you find out yourself. Status as of
**September 2026**:

| App | Actual status |
| --- | --- |
| **Sales** | **Live and in daily use.** It's where almost all of the product happens. |
| **Orders** | **Live.** The most used of the five operational apps, with daily movement. |
| **Inventory** | **It works, but it's among the least used.** Everything it promises — warehouses, movements, overselling — is operational. |
| **Calendar** | **It works, little used.** Appointments, services, availability rules and booking page are operational. |
| **Shipping** | **Half-built.** A single courier connected by API: **Shalom**. Any other one is recorded as a manual shipment. |
| **Electronic invoicing** | **Built, no mileage.** It takes for granted that you'll be among the first to use it: try it with a one-off receipt before depending on it. |

<Warning>
**If you're going to install Shipping, this changes what you expect of it.** The
connection with Shalom doesn't use an API key: it uses **your courier account
password**. You have to choose by hand the agency you dispatch from. And the
switch that generates the waybills **starts off on purpose**, because every
waybill is real and the courier charges it to your account. Today the connection
reads your Shalom account. Try it with a real shipment before trusting a batch
to it.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**If you're going to install Electronic invoicing: nothing is issued from its
screen.** There you only configure the issuer and look at the receipts already
issued. Today issuing comes in through a single path — a copilot proposal that
you approve — so don't look for an **Issue** button: it doesn't exist.
</Warning>

## What they cost

**Today only the Sales app charges a subscription.** Calendar, Inventory,
Orders, Shipping and Electronic invoicing show on their card as **Free**:
switching them on doesn't raise your bill.

<Warning>
**"Free" means "no subscription", not "no cost".** Whatever those apps consume
in artificial intelligence still comes off your balance, just like your agent's
conversations. Each one keeps its own meter.
</Warning>

The Sales arithmetic — the base, the agent quota and the US$ 50 that come back
to you as balance — is set out in full in **[Plans and
pricing](/en/getting-started/pricing)**.

<Note>
If you see "Sales" in some old text, it's the same app: **it's called Ventas**,
in the dashboard and on your receipt.
</Note>

## Where they're switched on and off

In **Administration → Apps**. The account owner does it: an invited employee
normally **doesn't even see that entry** in their menu.

Each app is a card with its status, its price and, if it's active, the month's
estimated charge.

| Status | What it means | Button you'll see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Active** | Switched on and in the menu. | **Deactivate** · **Uninstall** |
| **Deactivated** | Installed but switched off. It doesn't show in the menu. | **Activate** · **Uninstall** |
| **Archived** | Uninstalled. Its data is still stored. | **Install** |
| **Not installed** | You never had it. | **Install** |

<Check>
**No button acts on the first click.** Before installing, activating or
deactivating, a window opens showing you **what happens to your bill**, with the
before and the after. Uninstalling asks for its own confirmation. You can look
around without fear of breaking anything.
</Check>

<Note>
**Deactivating and uninstalling are not the same**, even though they look alike:

- **Deactivate** leaves the app installed and switched off. It's undone with one
  click on **Activate**.
- **Uninstall** archives it. It requires the app to be deactivated first, but
  the button chains the two steps for you.

And the three **Core** pieces allow neither of the two: they have no buttons.
</Note>

<Check>
**Uninstalling an app doesn't delete your data: it archives it.** If you install
it again, everything shows up as you left it. The panel itself tells you so in
its subtitle. Switching Inventory off doesn't make you lose your stock.
</Check>

<Warning>
**A rare case worth recognizing:** an app can show as **Active** and carry
alongside it a red pill that says **kill-switch**. It's an emergency brake
applied from our side, and it overrides the status: with that pill in place, the
app doesn't operate even though it says "Active". If you run into it, write to
us.
</Warning>

## Common problems

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The documentation mentions an entry I don't have in my menu">
    That's normal, and it's what this page is about. That app is **switched off
    in your business**. Open **Administration → Apps** and look at its card: if
    it says **Deactivated** or **Not installed**, there's your answer.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I went in by the address and got 'App not active'">
    It isn't a technical error: it's the screen that comes up when you type the
    address of an app that's switched off. If you administer the apps, it comes
    with a **Go to Apps** button. If not, it tells you to ask your administrator
    to activate it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="My employee gets an error opening Orders, but they can see the entry">
    It isn't a fault: **the menu shows an app because it's switched on in the
    business, not because you have permission to go in.** Permission is granted
    separately, person by person.

    Five of the six apps — Calendar, Orders, Inventory, Shipping and Electronic
    invoicing — require their own permission. **Sales is the exception: it has
    no permission of its own**, so anyone with access to the account gets into
    Sales, always within your business's data. If that matters to you, bear it
    in mind before inviting someone.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="My menu ran out of apps">
    If none are left switched on, the menu doesn't leave you a gap: it shows the
    owner an **Add apps** button that leads straight to the panel. With the
    sidebar shrunk to icons only that button doesn't appear — expand it first.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="On the phone the menu names are different">
    On screens of 768 px or less the sidebar turns into a bar of five buttons at
    the bottom: **Home**, three shortcuts and **More**. The middle three use
    short names and come from your active apps in order of importance. With the
    four apps a new account comes with, they are **Chats** (which on the desktop
    is Conversations), **Schedule** (Calendar) and **Orders**. Everything else
    lives inside **More**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The menu looks different on another computer">
    What the menu remembers about you — which submenus you left open and whether
    you shrank the bar — **is saved in your browser, not in your account**. On
    another machine, or after clearing the site's data, it goes back to its
    factory state. What does belong to the business, and travels with you, is
    which apps are switched on.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I shrank the sidebar and things disappeared">
    The bar has three shapes: **expanded** with the names, **shrunk** to icons
    only (with tooltips when you hover) and, in narrow windows, a **drawer**
    that overlays the page. In the shrunk shape there are no submenus, no
    **Administration** header and no **Add apps** button, and the agent selector
    moves up, next to **Home**.

    The button to change shape is right at the bottom: expanded it reads
    **Collapse**; shrunk only its icon is left, and on hover the tooltip says
    **Expand**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="There are screens I can't reach from the menu">
    There are two, and you get into them from inside:

    - **The Persona Studio**, where the agent's face and way of speaking are
      defined: it opens from the **Playbook** or from **Customize**.
    - **The schedule settings**: they open from the schedule selector on the
      **Calendar** screen (the gear next to it).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

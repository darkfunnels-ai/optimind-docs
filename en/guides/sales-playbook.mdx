The playbook is your agent's script. It is not internal documentation: it is
what your agent obeys, word for word, every time a customer writes. The screen
itself says so under the title: *«Un documento. Lo que lees es lo que el agente
obedece.»* (*"One document. What you read is what the agent obeys."*)

It is split into **chapters** — greet, qualify, offer, close — and each
customer's conversation lives in just one of them at a time.

The dashboard is in Spanish, so buttons, menus and messages are written here
exactly as you will see them on screen, with the English in parentheses the
first time.

<Note>
**Where it is.** Go to the dashboard at
[optimind.darkfunnels.ai](https://optimind.darkfunnels.ai) and, in the
left-hand menu, inside **Ventas** (*Sales*) → the **EMBUDO** (*Funnel*) block,
click **Manual** (*Playbook*). (If you prefer to go straight there:
`optimind.darkfunnels.ai/manual`.)

And the other way round: the **Guía** (*Guide*) button in the Playbook's bar
opens this very page in another tab.
</Note>

<Info>
**If you see a different screen instead of the playbook:**

- **«App no activa»** (*App not active*): the **Ventas** app is not switched on
  in your business. If you manage the account, the message comes with the
  **Ir a Apps** (*Go to Apps*) button to activate it; if not, it asks you to
  tell whoever manages it.
- **«No tienes acceso a Ventas»** (*You don't have access to Ventas*): the app
  is active, but your user does not have permission to use it. Permission is
  given from **Personas y accesos** (*People and access*):
  [Invite your team](/en/getting-started/invite-your-team) explains how.
</Info>

<Warning>
**The script is written on a computer.** On screens under 900 px wide (the
phone, and also many tablets held upright) the playbook opens **read-only**:
you can read it, **touch up data** (prices, phone numbers, links), **test it**
and see the **measured changes**, but not rewrite it.
[From the phone](#from-the-phone) covers it.
</Warning>

<Note>
**Two words that are not the same thing.** **The copilot** is the AI that lives
inside the dashboard and proposes changes to you. **Your assistant** is Claude,
ChatGPT or Codex connected from outside: it is optional, and it mostly shows up
at the end of this page, in «What each thing is called outside the editor» and
«From your assistant».

And a third: if in an old video you hear **«etapa»** (*stage*), it is the same
thing as **capítulo** (*chapter*). It was renamed.
</Note>

## What reaches your agent in every message

Of the chapters, **only one reaches it**: the one that conversation is in.
(The exception is a jump marked «ejecuta ya» — *run now*: in that same message
the destination chapter reaches it too.) What you wrote in chapter 4 does not
exist for it while the conversation is in chapter 1. If a rule has to hold in
all of them, write it in every chapter where it matters — or put it in a
**shared section §**, which you will see further down.

The chapter does not travel alone. Along with it, on every turn, go:

- **the active persona** (if none is chosen, nothing from the persona travels),
- **the customer's data**, their notes and their appointments,
- **the style rules** for writing on WhatsApp,
- the **tools** and the **files** of that chapter,
- and, if the playbook uses **business details** (your Yape, your account, your
  social media), the value they have today in Configuración (*Settings*).

## One change, start to finish

This is the complete cycle. The rest of the page explains each step in detail.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the funnel">
    In the left-hand menu, right below the **Ventas** heading, is the funnel
    selector: your funnel's photo or initial and its name. Choose the funnel
    whose playbook you want to change and click **Manual**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the change">
    Click the line you want to change and type. To name a file, a tool, a piece
    of data or a chapter, type **`@`** (see
    [The «@» menu](#the-menu-mentioning-things)).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the Doctor">
    If the **Doctor** button carries a number, open it. They are verified
    problems in your text, each one with its fix or with **Ir al sitio** (*Go
    to the spot*).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Guardar cambios** (*Save changes*). **Until then, your agent carries
    on with the previous playbook.**
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test it">
    Click **Probar** (*Test*) and write to it as if you were a customer. It
    answers with the playbook you have just saved, and nothing goes out over
    WhatsApp.
  </Step>
  <Step title="See how it did">
    When the change has finished being measured — the row itself tells you
    which day —, **Cambios medidos** (*Measured changes*) tells you whether
    conversations went better or worse since that change, and lets you go back
    to how it was.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### The index on the left

It lists your chapters with their number and their name, and after them the

- **＋ Nuevo capítulo** (*New chapter*)
- **＋ Sección compartida** (*Shared section*)
- **＋ Insertar patrón** (*Insert pattern*) — which also opens by **typing `/`

- **＋ Cargar plantilla de Ecommerce** (*Load the Ecommerce template*) — only in

<Tip>
**«＋ Insertar patrón» saves you writing the script from scratch.** It brings

</Tip>

The whole index **does not appear on the phone**.

### The document

Right at the top, the title **«Manual del agente»** (*Agent playbook*) and the
sentence that sums up why it exists. Below it, the **persona** pill (with its
photo or its initial, and its name) and, in funnels created with the new system,
the **«Memoria a Largo Plazo (RAG)»** (*Long-Term Memory (RAG)*) button. Then
the chapters begin, and after them the shared sections §.

On a computer, the **Recursos** drawer opens by itself when you go in. If you
close it, the document gains width.

## Inside a chapter

The whole document is the editor: you click a line and type. Inside the
document there are no forms and no labeled fields. This is what you see in each
chapter:

**The number and the name.** On the left, the label **«Capítulo 3»** (*Chapter
3*); next to it, the name, which you write. The same number appears in the
index on the left. If you leave the name blank, the chapter stays **unnamed**:
in the index you will see «(sin nombre)» (*(no name)*) and your agent will only
receive its number. It is worth giving it a short one.

<Tip>
**Do not put the number in the name.** Write «Calificación» (*Qualification*),
not «2. Calificación». In the chapter heading and in the index the name is
shown **exactly as you write it**, so you would see «Capítulo 2» and «2.
Calificación» side by side, repeated. In the `@` menu and in the lists for
choosing a chapter, that leading number is trimmed automatically.
</Tip>

**The chapter's lines.** This is the script: what your agent executes. **It
carries no label inside the editor**; they are simply the lines you write under
the name. It is the part you will touch the most. To add a line at the end,
hover over the chapter: a thin **«＋ añadir instrucción»** (*add instruction*)
line appears; clicking it turns it into the box **«＋ Escribe una instrucción —
teclea @ para mencionar»** (*Write an instruction — type @ to mention*) (in an
empty chapter and on touch screens it is visible without hovering).

**«Tu papel aquí:»** (*Your role here:*). This is who your agent is in that
chapter. On a computer it appears in every chapter, even if it is empty, with
the hint *«(opcional) el papel propio de este capítulo»* (*(optional) this
chapter's own role*). On the phone it only shows if it has something written.

<Note>
**If you remember a «Quién eres» (*Who you are*) block at the very top, it no
longer exists.** Previously, if all the chapters said the same thing about who
the agent is, the editor gathered it at the top. Since September 2026 the role
is shown inside its own chapter. What your agent receives did not change: only
where it is drawn.
</Note>

**The execution chip**, next to the name: it says **«se ejecuta cada vez»**
(*runs every time*) or **«se ejecuta 1 vez → pasa a capítulo N»** (*runs once →
goes to chapter N*). It is explained further down.

**The header buttons.** So as not to crowd the row, only the execution chip and
the **⋯** menu stay in view. Hovering over the chapter brings up two more short
buttons, and the **⋯** holds the rest:

- **«Ver»** (*View*) — opens a window with that chapter's text as it will be
  saved.
- **«✦ Reescribir»** (*Rewrite*) — selects the whole chapter and opens the
  ✦ IA (*AI*) actions.
- In the **⋯** menu: **«Ver como lo recibe el agente»** (*See it as the agent
  receives it*), **«✦ Reescribir el capítulo»** (*Rewrite the chapter*),
  **«Probar desde aquí»** (*Test from here*; it starts the test chat at that
  chapter), **«Ayúdame a completarlo»** (*Help me complete it*; only in a
  template funnel, if that chapter has placeholders left) and **«Eliminar
  capítulo»** (*Delete chapter*; read [Deleting a chapter](#deleting-a-chapter)
  before using it).

On touch screens there is no hovering: the buttons stay in view.

**The «Le aplican:» (*Applied here:*) footer.** At the end of each chapter, the
list of shared sections § added to it. A click on one takes you to it.

<Note>
**«Ver como lo recibe el agente» shows your text, not the complete message.**
It is what will be saved, word for word, with the mentions already converted —
including the changes you have not saved yet. Very useful for checking what you
have just written. But when it is actually sent, the system adds on top of it
the persona, the customer's data, the labels for each part, the tools and the
files. The § sections have that button too.
</Note>

### Lines color themselves

Don't be alarmed if you see colors you did not put there: the editor detects
each line's intent by how it starts. Those beginning with **«Si…»** (*If…*)
are painted as a condition, and those beginning with **«Nunca…»** (*Never…*)
or **«Siempre…»** (*Always…*) as a fixed rule. It also works after a heading:
«PASO 2: Si el cliente insiste…» (*STEP 2: If the customer insists…*) gets
colored. It is a visual aid in the editor: the text that gets saved is exactly
the one you wrote.

### The index as a map

On the left, each chapter carries a **colored dot**: red if the Doctor found an
error in it, amber if it has warnings, green if there is nothing. Hover over the
row and the tooltip tells you what is wrong with it; a click on the dot takes
you to the line of the first finding. Under the «Manual» label the total is
summarized («1 error · 2 avisos» — *1 error · 2 warnings*).

Also:

- **«1×»** marks the chapters that run only once.
- **«vacío»** (*empty*) marks those with no lines of their own (only the §
  sections apply to them).
- **«N por completar»** (*N to complete*) marks, in a funnel from the Ecommerce
  template, the chapters and sections that still have placeholders to fill.

### Collapsing chapters

With six chapters the document gets long. The chevron to the left of each label
**collapses** the chapter: what is left is the heading and one line summarizing
it (how many PASOS it has, how many files and tools it mentions, which chapters
it exits to and how many § sections apply to it). A click on the chevron or on
that summary opens it again. In the index, **«Plegar todo»** (*Collapse all*)
collapses every chapter at once and **«Desplegar todo»** (*Expand all*) opens
them.

Collapsing does not touch the text: what is written is still there, it just
stops being visible. And any jump (a chapter from the index, the Doctor's «Ir
al sitio», the first use of a resource, the chapter the test chat walks
through) expands the chapter before taking you there.

## The shared sections §

A **shared section** is a block marked with the **§** symbol that applies to
several chapters at once. You write it once and it serves all the ones you
choose. It carries a chip saying which ones it applies to: **«aplica a
capítulos 1–2, 4»** (*applies to chapters 1–2, 4* — consecutive numbers are
joined with a dash) or **«sin capítulos»** (*no chapters*) if it applies to
none. On a computer, a click on that chip lets you change the scope.

<Note>
**For you it is one block; for your agent they are copies.** On saving, the
section's text is **copied in full inside each chapter in its scope**. If it
applies to four chapters, it is written four times — that is why playbooks
weigh what they weigh. And a section that applies to no chapter **is not copied
into any of them**: your agent does not see it.
</Note>

<Warning>
**«PUNTOS CLAVE:» (*KEY POINTS:*) is not decoration: it is structure.** Inside
a section, that literal line separates knowledge from rules, and **it has to go
at the start of the text or right after a blank line**, with what follows on
the line below. If you delete it or put it in the middle of a paragraph, the
system stops telling the two apart and treats it all as context.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Emptying a § section that does apply to chapters blocks your save, and just
as well.** If you delete its content and press save, the system **does not
save** and explains why: saving it empty would erase that context in every
chapter where it applies. To get out of it, undo with `⌘Z`, write something
inside, or delete it from its **⋯** menu → **«Eliminar sección»** (*Delete
section*). The notice names the first empty section it finds, even if there
are several.

That safety net **does not cover** sections without scope (the ones saying
«sin capítulos»): the Doctor warns you about those.
</Warning>

<Tip>
If you have two § sections that say almost the same thing, the editor points it
out above the document — **«Copias casi idénticas (no colapsan)»**
(*Near-identical copies (they do not collapse)*) — and, on a computer, brings a
wizard to unify them with the **«Unificar →»** (*Unify*) button. It is a
frequent problem in playbooks that have been around a while.
</Tip>

## Writing the script: the PASOS (steps)

**«PASO 1:», «PASO 2:»… (*STEP 1:, STEP 2:…*) are not a button or a field: they
are a way of writing** inside the chapter's lines. Your agent understands it,
and so does the system: when a scheduled reminder has to start at PASO 4, it
trims the text so it starts right there.

```
PASO 1: Saluda por su nombre y pregunta qué producto le interesa.
PASO 2: Si pide precio, dile esto exactamente: «...»
PASO 3: Cuando tengas el distrito, pasa al PASO 4.
PASO 4: Confirma el pedido y Llama a la herramienta advance_chapter con el capítulo 3
```

*(STEP 1: Greet them by name and ask which product they are interested in.
STEP 2: If they ask for the price, tell them this exactly: «...» STEP 3: When
you have the district, go to STEP 4. STEP 4: Confirm the order and call the
advance_chapter tool with chapter 3.)*

- Each step carries its action and its exit («Si …», «Cuando tengas X, salta al
  PASO N» — *If …*, *When you have X, jump to STEP N*).
- If the chapter **takes a different path** depending on the customer's
  situation (typical of the first one), the condition goes **inside the step it
  affects**, not in a paragraph before PASO 1. For example: «PASO 2: Si el
  cliente ya dijo qué busca, sáltate este paso y ve al PASO 3» (*STEP 2: If the
  customer already said what they are looking for, skip this step and go to
  STEP 3*).
- Scripts that must come out verbatim go with **«dile esto:»** or **«dile esto
  exactamente:»** (*tell them this:* / *tell them this exactly:*).

<Warning>
**The trimming only works if «PASO 4» starts the line and the number is in
digits.** Written «Paso cuatro» (*Step four*), or halfway through a sentence,
the system does not find it — and then it sends your agent **the whole
chapter** from the beginning. It does not fail loudly: it fails by doing more
than it should.
</Warning>

### The «✦ IA» pill: let the AI tidy up your text

You do not have to structure it by hand. Select a piece of the chapter and
click the **«✦ IA»** (*AI*) pill that appears above it:

| Action | What it proposes |
| --- | --- |
| **Estructurar en pasos** (*Structure into steps*) | The messy text, turned into PASO 1 / PASO 2 / … |
| **Acortar sin perder datos** (*Shorten without losing data*) | The same information in fewer words. |
| **Extraer condiciones** (*Extract conditions*) | The rules in the text, as «Si…» and «Nunca…» lines. |

The proposal arrives as a comparison: **nothing changes until you click
«Aceptar»** (*Accept*), and nothing reaches the server until you save. These
three actions use your account's balance.

In the same menu **«Enviar en bloque»** (*Send as a block*) may appear, which
does not use AI (see «A passage that goes out as-is», in
[Sending a file from the script](#sending-a-file-from-the-script)) and, if the
selection has a wrongly written tool, **«Arreglar herramienta»** (*Fix tool*).

## Making the conversation advance

There are two ways for the conversation to move from one chapter to another.
The usual one is **a mark inside the chapter's text**, written in the script's
lines. The other is the «se ejecuta 1 vez» chip, which jumps to the chosen
chapter as soon as that chapter finishes; it is explained further down in this
same section.

**The convenient way:** type **`@`**, choose the **Capítulos** (*Chapters*)
group and choose the destination chapter. A **mention** is left on the line
(that colored "ink" of the editor). One click on it changes it; `⌫` deletes it.

**The literal way,** which is what you will see written in old playbooks and
what you can type by hand:

```
Llama a la herramienta advance_chapter con el capítulo 3
```

*(Call the advance_chapter tool with chapter 3.)*

A conditional version also works:

```
Si el cliente acepta la recomendación, Llama a la herramienta advance_chapter con el capítulo 4
```

*(If the customer accepts the recommendation, call the advance_chapter tool with
chapter 4.)*

<Warning>
**Writing it in prose is not reliable.** Your agent is instructed to use the
advance tool only when the literal line asks it to. That is why, with «Avanza
al capítulo 5» (*Advance to chapter 5*), «pasa al siguiente» (*go to the next
one*) or «continúa» (*continue*), the usual outcome is that the conversation
stays stuck in that chapter, with no visible error. It is one of
the most expensive and most silent failures. In the in-between chapters, the
Doctor does detect it: **«El capítulo N no avanza a ningún capítulo»**
(*Chapter N does not advance to any chapter*).
</Warning>

Details worth knowing:

- **The destination has to exist.** If there is no chapter with that number —
  for example, if your agent tries to advance beyond the last one —, the
  advance is rejected and the conversation stays where it was. The Doctor
  detects mentions of chapters that no longer exist.
- **The advance is applied when the reply is delivered.** A turn that gets
  interrupted (the customer writes over it) does not advance.

### Jumping within the same message

Normally the jump happens and your agent waits for the customer's next reply.
If you want it to execute the destination chapter **in that same message**,
there is a mark for that:

**Click a chapter mention that is already written** and the checkbox
**«Ejecutar ese capítulo de inmediato»** (*Execute that chapter immediately*)
appears, with its explanation: *«en el mismo turno, sin esperar a que el
cliente escriba»* (*in the same turn, without waiting for the customer to
write*). With the checkbox ticked, the mention says so: **«· ejecuta ya»** (*·
runs now*).

<Note>
**That checkbox is not there when you insert the mention for the first time**,
only when you click an existing one to change it. Every new mention is born
without the mark. Written by hand, the equivalent is adding **«y ejecútalo en
este mismo mensaje»** (*and execute it in this same message*) at the end of the
advance line. The ceiling is **3 chained jumps** in a single message.
</Note>

### Chapters that run only once

The greeting is the typical case: it is said once and it moves on to the next
one. That is handled from the **chip next to the chapter's name**, which says
**«se ejecuta cada vez»** or **«se ejecuta 1 vez → pasa a capítulo N»**.

Clicking it opens a small window — **«Ejecución de «Saludo»»** (*Execution of
"Greeting"*), with your chapter's name — with two controls:

1. The checkbox **«Se ejecuta una sola vez»** (*Runs only once*).
2. The list **«al terminar pasa a»** (*on finishing, go to*), whose default
   option is **«siguiente (capítulo N+1)»** (*next (chapter N+1)*).

The list is **disabled until you tick the checkbox**, and if you untick the
checkbox the destination is erased.

<Note>
**That chip can only be clicked in the full editor** (`/manual`, on a
computer). From the phone, or from the playbook embedded in Conversaciones
(*Conversations*), the chip does not respond to the click and, on top of that,
it is only visible when the chapter is set to «una sola vez» (*only once*): if
it says «se ejecuta cada vez», nothing is shown there.
</Note>

### Notifying someone by name

The **Avisar a un tercero** (*Notify a third party*) tool (your agent sends a
WhatsApp message to someone else, like the warehouse manager) has a field for
saying **to whom**. There, type `@` or press **＋** and choose:

- **Equipo de ventas** (*Sales team*): your **«Informar de las ventas»**
  (*Report sales*) list from Configuración.
- Any contact from your **Números frecuentes**.

The playbook stores **the name, not the number**:
`Dile a «Equipo de ventas» que …` (*Tell "Sales team" that …*). That way, if
the number changes, you change it in Configuración and the playbook is not
touched. If you do not have any saved contacts yet, the form reminds you. A
number typed by hand also works, with its country code.

## Sending a file from the script

For your agent to send a file from your Library you write a **marker** at the
exact point in the script where it should go out. Inside it goes the file's
**nickname** (its send trigger), exactly as it appears in the Library:

```
PASO 2: Si pide ver el catálogo, envíalo aquí: ###SEND_FILES: catalogo###
```

*(STEP 2: If they ask to see the catalog, send it here: ###SEND_FILES:
catalogo###)*

**You do not have to write it by hand** — and it is better not to. You have
three ways of inserting it correctly placed: the **Recursos** drawer in the
bar, the **`@` → Archivos** menu, or the **✦ ¿Dónde?** (*Where?*) button in
Recursos, which asks the AI for the sentence with the marker already inside.

- **The default nickname is the file's name without the extension**, and it is
  the one worth keeping: that way the marker, your agent's search and the `@`
  menu all name the same thing. Files linked a long time ago may keep a
  different, older nickname.
- **If the marker is written correctly, the customer does not see it**: it is
  replaced by the file. If it is missing a `#` sign, on the other hand, **that
  text goes out as-is in the chat**; and if the nickname does not resolve, the
  marker disappears and the message goes out with no attachment, with no
  visible error.
- **The editor's red underline compares against the nicknames**, not against
  the file names: a marker written with the name may come out underlined and
  still be saved and work. If you leave nickname = name, the two match and the
  doubt is over.
- **Every file mention carries a status dot** in the editor: green if the file
  is active, amber if it is **paused** in the Library (it exists, but the agent
  will not send it even if the playbook asks for it) and red if it does not
  exist. Hover over the mention and a card shows the file (type and size), its
  status, the condition that triggers it and which other chapters use it; from
  there you can **view the file**, **swap it for another**, **reactivate it** if
  it is paused or, if it does not exist, **reconnect it** to another one or
  open Recursos to upload it.
- **If you take away the file's send permission**, the markers that named it
  are left with nothing to resolve. The playbook's text is not touched by
  itself: the Doctor points it out to you.

Everything else — how a file is uploaded, how the permission is granted, what
happens if two files share a nickname and why the same file is not re-sent for
6 hours — lives in [The file Library](/en/guides/library).

### A passage that goes out as-is

Your agent splits its reply into several short bubbles. If you have a text that
must go out whole and untouched — some payment details, some terms —, wrap it.
Select it and choose **«Enviar en bloque»** in the **✦ IA** pill (or in the
`@` menu → **Envío**), and the editor puts in the markers:

```
###BLOCK###
Datos para el depósito:
Banco — cuenta 000-0000000
A nombre de: Moda Sol
###/BLOCK###
```

*(Deposit details: Bank — account 000-0000000. Account name: Moda Sol.)*

That passage goes out in **a single bubble, verbatim**, with its line breaks.
The ceiling is 4,000 characters per block and 3 blocks per reply; anything over
that, or a block that does not close, goes out as normal text. The Doctor
detects unclosed blocks.

<Tip>
**If your agent copies the passage but forgets the markers**, the system
recognizes the passage and sends it in a single bubble anyway, without changing
a word. If instead of copying it, it retells it in other words, it goes out
split up as normal text.
</Tip>

<Note>
If you have your products in the [catalog](/en/guides/catalog), your agent can
look the price up there: that way you do not have to repeat it in the
playbook. If you also write it in the playbook, the Doctor warns you when it
does not match the catalog's. And your payment details work better as business
details (see [The «@» menu](#the-menu-mentioning-things)).
</Note>

## The «Datos» (Data) panel

The **Datos** button in the bar opens a panel titled **«Datos duros»** (*Hard
data*) that gathers, in a single list, the playbook's **links · prices · phone
numbers · exact phrases**. It is the answer to "my price changed, where do I
change it?" without having to reread the whole playbook.

- Each value says how many times it appears and in which chapters. A click
  takes you to the first appearance. At the top you have a search box,
  **«Buscar valor…»** (*Search value…*).
- The **links, prices and phone numbers** come with an **«Actualizar»**
  (*Update*) button. It opens a window where you write the **new value** and
  choose which appearances to apply it to (all of them come ticked). It
  finishes with **«Actualizar N»** (*Update N*).
- The **exact phrases** are only listed: you can jump to them and edit them by
  hand, but **they do not have an «Actualizar» button**.
- If the same product appears with different prices, or the same link with
  variants, the panel warns you at the top.

Like everything in the editor, the change stays pending until you click
**«Guardar cambios»**.

<Note>
**"All the appearances" has one exception, and the system tells you about it.**
If changing a value would break a mention in the playbook, that appearance is
skipped and a notice comes up along the lines of *«N apariciones se omitieron:
el nuevo valor alteraría una mención del manual»* (*N appearances were skipped:
the new value would alter a playbook mention*). When you see it, go to those
lines by hand.
</Note>

On the phone this panel is the door to **«Retocar datos»** (*Touch up data*):
there it shows only prices, phone numbers and links.
[From the phone](#from-the-phone) covers it.

## The agent's persona

<Note>
**"Persona" here is not a human being.** It is the identity your agent adopts:
name, job title, face and voice. It is what makes it introduce itself as
"Valentina, from Moda Sol" and not as a robot.
</Note>

A new funnel is born with its agent's persona already created and **active**,
with a name and a tone. At the top of the playbook there is a pill with its photo (or
its initial) and its name. If the agent has no active persona, the pill says
so: **«No hay persona seleccionada»** (*No persona selected*).

Clicking it opens the **«Persona activa»** (*Active persona*) panel. Each
persona appears with:

- its **name** and its **job title** (or **«Sin cargo»** — *No job title*),
- two marks, **«Cara»** (*Face*) and **«Voz»** (*Voice*), which light up if it
  has a photo and a voice configured,
- the label **«Activa»** (*Active*) on the chosen one, and an **«Activar»**
  (*Activate*) button on the others,
- and an **«Editar»** (*Edit*) button.

**«Editar»** opens the persona editor right there, without leaving the playbook
(**«← Volver a Personas»** — *Back to Personas* — takes you back to the list).
**«Nueva persona»** (*New persona*), at the top, creates another one. And
**«Gestionar Personas →»** (*Manage Personas*), at the bottom, opens the full
Personas screen.

What travels in your agent's message is the **name, the job title, the
personality and the context** of the active persona. The **face** and the
**voice** are used by the avatar and the voice notes.

<Note>
**If you delete the funnel, its persona is deleted with it**, unless another
funnel has it assigned. Personas you created by hand and did not assign to
anyone are not touched.
</Note>

### Long-Term Memory (RAG)

Next to the persona there may be a second button, **«Memoria a Largo Plazo
(RAG)»** (*Long-Term Memory (RAG)*). It opens a knowledge document separate
from the playbook, with a ceiling of **200,000 characters**. Your agent
consults it with the **Consultar conocimiento (RAG)** (*Consult knowledge
(RAG)*) tool when a customer asks something.

The difference from the playbook, in one line: **the playbook is orders; the
memory is knowledge.** What your agent must *do* goes in the playbook; what it
must *know* in order to answer questions, in the memory.

Inside it has:

- **Editar** (*Edit*) and **Vista previa** (*Preview*): the document accepts
  headings, bold and lists (Markdown format), and the bar at the top puts them
  in for you.
- **Probar** (*Test*): a search box where you write a question *the way a
  customer would* and see what your agent would receive. It helps you check
  whether something is already written before writing it again. What you have
  just saved can take up to a minute to be found by meaning; in the meantime,
  the search finds it by the exact words.
- **Its own Guardar (*Save*) button**, separate from the playbook's: it saves
  instantly and tells you how many fragments it ended up in. Saving the
  document empty withdraws the memory: your agent stops consulting it.

You can also ask the copilot for it: «agrega a la memoria nuestra política de
cambios» (*add our exchange policy to the memory*). The copilot adds a section
to the document and saves it without asking for your approval. You will see it
here, and you can edit it like any other.

That button **only appears in funnels created with the new system**. If your
funnel is an old one, you will not see it.

<Warning>
**If your funnel is deactivated**, the persona and the memory can be seen but
not changed: the **«Activar»** button comes up grayed out, **«Nueva persona»**
does not appear and the memory says **«Solo lectura»** (*Read-only*) instead of
Guardar.
</Warning>

## How it saves

<Warning>
**The playbook does not save itself to the server.** You have to press the
button. All that happens on its own is a copy in your own browser.
</Warning>

The button on the right tells you where you stand:

| What it says | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Guardar** (*Save*, grayed out) | There is nothing to save. It is not broken: it is up to date. |
| **Guardar cambios** (*Save changes*) | You have pending changes. It carries a dot next to it. |
| **Guardando…** (*Saving…*) | It is writing. |
| **Guardado** ✓ (*Saved*) | It went in. The tick goes away by itself in under two seconds. |

And if you have second thoughts before saving, **«Descartar cambios»** (which
only appears when there is something to discard) returns the document to the
last saved version.

If on saving the system sees that your change creates a new structural problem
in the PASOS, the **Doctor opens by itself** so you see it before you leave.
Problems that were already there before do not open it: you see those in the
button's number.

### The local copy: a net from one day to the next

While you write, the editor saves a copy **in your browser**: half a second
after you stop typing, and instantly when you create or delete a chapter. It is
there so you do not lose the work if you reload the page or go to another
screen: when you come back, your changes are still there and **«Guardar
cambios»** is still lit. If it is the first time you open the Playbook since
you opened or reloaded the dashboard, the notice **«Todavía tienes cambios en
el manual sin guardar»** (*You still have unsaved changes in the playbook*)
also comes up, with **Seguir editando** (*Keep editing*) and **Guardar**
(*Save*).

<Warning>
**That copy is not a backup.** It lives only in that browser (it is not on
another computer), it expires after **7 days** and it is discarded by itself if
the playbook changed on the server in the meantime — because someone else saved
it, or because you saved it from the chat panel. In that case the local draft
is lost: the system would rather lose it than write over someone else's work.
</Warning>

### One save, one version

**Every time you press save, ONE version is created in the Historial**, even if
you changed ten chapters at once. If you did not really change anything, no
version is created.

These also create a version: saving from the Conversaciones panel or from your
assistant, applying a copilot proposal, loading the Ecommerce template and
**«Volver a como estaba»** (*Go back to how it was*) from Cambios medidos.

**The Historial keeps only the 10 most recent versions**: number 11 pushes the
oldest off the list.

<Tip>
Hence the hygiene rule that matters most: **batch your changes and save in
rounds**. Five chapters saved one by one burn five of your ten slots in an
afternoon; the same five in one save burn one.
</Tip>

## The History: rolling back

The **Historial** button (on a computer) opens a window titled **«Historial del
embudo»** (*Funnel history*). There you see the saved versions, the **most
recent** at the top, with when each one was saved and how many chapters it had.

<Frame caption="The «Historial del embudo» (Funnel history): each saved version with its date, how long ago it was saved and how many chapters it had. Here there is only one, the most recent.">
  <img src="/images/manual/06-historial.png" alt="Historial del embudo (Funnel history) window with one version, 12 oct, 22:51, marked más reciente (most recent), hace 2 h · 3 capítulos (2 h ago · 3 chapters), and the note Se conservan las últimas 10 versiones por embudo (The last 10 versions per funnel are kept)" />
</Frame>

Choosing one opens the comparison: **Actual** (*Current*) against **Versión**
(*Version*), chapter by chapter. **Restoring is not all or nothing:** you can
accept only the pieces you are interested in, chapter by chapter, or use
**Aceptar todo** (*Accept all*) / **Descartar todo** (*Discard all*).

<Note>
**Restoring from the Historial does not write anything by itself.** It leaves
the changes in the editor, as if you had written them, and you have to click
**«Guardar cambios»** for them to go in. You can look at them without fear of
breaking anything: if they do not convince you, **Descartar cambios** leaves it
as it was. Careful: **Probar** answers with the saved playbook, so to test what
you restored you have to save it first.
</Note>

In the Historial — and in the review of the copilot's proposals — the chapter's
parts appear under other names: **Rol** (*Role*), **Cadena de pensamiento**
(*Chain of thought*) and **Contexto** (*Context*). They are the same pieces you
see in the editor without a label.

## Measured changes: what happened after each change

You changed the greeting — did you sell more or less? **Cambios medidos**
(*Measured changes*) tells you. Every change you save is measured on its own:
the dashboard compares the week before with the week after and gives you a
reading. If the change went badly, you can return the playbook to how it was
with one button.

<Frame caption="The «Cambios medidos» (Measured changes) window for a funnel that has none yet: once you save a change, its before and after will show up here.">
  <img src="/images/manual/05-cambios-medidos.png" alt="Empty Cambios medidos (Measured changes) window, with the text Aún no hay cambios medidos en este embudo (No measured changes in this funnel yet)" />
</Frame>

**How a «change» is counted.** Saves of the same funnel less than 24 hours
apart count as **a single change**. Each row says the day, how many saves make
it up and its status.

**What it compares.** Up to 7 days before and up to 7 days after the change
(fewer if you made another change in between), with the same ads on both sides:

| Measure | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Respondieron al primer mensaje** (*Replied to the first message*) | What share of customers answered your agent's first message. |
| **Siguieron conversando** (*Kept talking*) | What share kept talking afterwards. |
| **Ventas en 48 h** (*Sales within 48 h*) | It is shown, but with the note *«todavía no dice nada»* (*it doesn't tell you anything yet*). |

**The statuses you will see:**

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Mejoró** / **Empeoró** / **Sin cambio claro** (*Improved / Got worse / No clear change*) | The reading is in. It is *«una señal, no una prueba»* (*a signal, not proof*). |
| **Midiendo** (*Measuring*) | Not finished yet. The row tells you on which day the reading comes out. |
| **Lo volviste a cambiar** (*You changed it again*) | You made another change before it could be measured. |
| **Poco tráfico** (*Low traffic*) | Too few conversations to measure it. |
| **No se puede leer** (*Can't be read*) | That week your line failed, something else about your agent also changed (the model, the persona, the settings) or your ads changed: your change cannot be separated out. |

The reading changes nothing by itself. As the window itself says: *«si no haces
nada, no cambia nada»* (*if you do nothing, nothing changes*).

### Going back to how it was

Expand the change's row and click **«Volver a como estaba el …»** (*Go back to
how it was on …*, with the date). It asks you to confirm: your agent will use
that day's playbook **from its next message**, and your persona and your
funnel's data do not change. If you had unsaved changes in the playbook, they
are discarded.

- The rollback **creates a new version** in the Historial, and it is measured
  like any other change.
- **It also works from the phone**: it is not an edit of the script, the server
  does it.
- It requires the same permission as saving the playbook.

The button does not appear, or the rollback is rejected, in these cases (the
row tells you which):

- after that change **you made others** — to choose what to recover, use the
  [Historial](#the-history-rolling-back);
- the change **did not touch the text of any chapter**;
- **you already rolled it back**;
- **that version is no longer stored**;
- **that version has no chapters**;
- going back **would delete a chapter where there are customers today**, or one
  they have a pending advance towards: move them first;
- your referrals chapter or a shipping rule **points to a chapter the rollback
  would delete**;
- **you do not have permission** to change the playbook.

## What the copilot proposes to you

When you ask the copilot to change your playbook, it does not change it on its
own: it leaves you a **proposal**. Proposals for playbook changes that are
waiting for your decision appear in a strip above the document — on the phone
too —: *«El copiloto propuso cambios en el manual y esperan tu decisión»* (*The
copilot proposed changes to the playbook and they are waiting for your
decision*), one row per proposal.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the proposal">
    Click **«Revisar y elegir»** (*Review and choose*). **«Revisar propuesta del
    copiloto»** (*Review the copilot's proposal*) opens, with your current text
    and the proposed one, change by change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose what to apply">
    For each change you choose **«Aplicar este cambio»** (*Apply this change*)
    or **«Mantener actual»** (*Keep current*).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply">
    Click **«Aplicar N cambios»** (*Apply N changes*). Unlike the Historial,
    **applying here does save**: the copilot writes the changes and leaves a new
    version in the Historial. The playbook refreshes by itself.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If it does not convince you, **«Rechazar»** (*Reject*) discards it.

If the copilot proposes **the whole funnel** to you, that proposal does not
appear in the strip: it is approved in its own chat and replaces the complete
playbook. The one you had stays in the [Historial](#the-history-rolling-back).

<Note>
**With unsaved changes, save yours first.** The strip says so — *«guarda los
tuyos antes de revisarlos: aplicar encima de una edición sin guardar termina en
conflicto»* (*save yours before reviewing them: applying on top of an unsaved
edit ends in a conflict*) — and instead of «Revisar y elegir» it offers you
**Guardar cambios**. Proposals that went stale because the playbook changed
afterwards are withdrawn by themselves.
</Note>

If you asked for the proposal yourself, you will also see it in the
**«Pendientes»** (*Pending*) card on your **Inicio** (*Home*).

## The Doctor

The **Doctor** button reviews the whole playbook and lists what is broken, with
the number of errors and warnings in view. It is not AI and not opinion: they
are *«problemas verificados del manual, con su arreglo»* (*verified playbook
problems, with their fix*), as the button itself says. It checks while you
write, without waiting for you to save.

<Frame caption="The «Doctor del manual» (Playbook Doctor) panel with 0 errors and 16 warnings: each warning with its explanation and its «Ir al sitio» (Go to the spot) button.">
  <img src="/images/manual/03-doctor.png" alt="Doctor del manual (Playbook Doctor) panel with 0 errors and 16 warnings of the kind El capítulo N no es alcanzable (Chapter N is not reachable), each with the Ir al sitio (Go to the spot) button" />
</Frame>

Each finding brings its explanation; **«Ir al sitio»**, which takes you to the
chapter and, when there is one, to the exact line; and, if there is a safe fix,
a one-click button: **«Reconectar a «…»»** (*Reconnect to "…"*),
**«Formatear como «…»»** (*Format as "…"*), **«Renumerar PASOS»** (*Renumber
steps*), **«Poner … (precio del catálogo)»** (*Set … (catalog price)*),
**«Eliminar §»** (*Delete §*)…

What it checks, grouped:

- **The script's structure.** Chapters not written in PASOS; PASOS out of order
  or not starting at 1 (renumbered in one click, references included);
  references to a PASO that does not exist; in-between chapters that advance to
  no chapter; chapters that cannot be reached; gaps in the numbering; chapters
  with the same name; and a rule that would hold for the whole conversation
  stuck inside a chapter (that one goes in **PUNTOS CLAVE** of the shared
  section).
- **Mentions.** Broken mentions (a file or a variable that does not exist),
  mentions of a chapter that no longer exists, chapter markers in the old
  format, tools written without formatting or with their data outside the
  mention, unclosed send blocks and § sections without scope.
- **Prices.** A price in the playbook that differs from your catalog's for that
  product. (It is checked when the catalog is loaded.)
- **Business details.** A mention of a detail that does not exist in
  Configuración (error) or of one without a value (warning).
- **Notices to third parties.** A number the tool is going to reject (no country
  code, spaces inside…) and a notice by name to someone who is not in your
  Números frecuentes. In both cases, the notice does not go out.

<Note>
**The Doctor's fixes are not saved to the playbook by themselves.** They stay
as pending changes in the editor until you click **«Guardar cambios»**.
</Note>

### Data written by hand

In its own section, the Doctor shows you **your business's numbers, accounts,
social media and links written inside the playbook**: a payment line, the
account holder, an @username, a link. They are not wrong, but it is worth
taking them out of the text:

- a **detail of your own** written by hand → **«Pasar a Datos del negocio»**
  (*Move to Business details*): it creates the detail in Configuración with that
  value and puts its mention where it was written;
- a **notice to the number of someone you already have saved** →
  **«Avisar a «…» por nombre»** (*Notify "…" by name*).

With more than one, **«Pasar todos (N)»** (*Move all (N)*) moves them at once.
That way they change in one place and, if you share the funnel, they do not
travel.

Watch the order: **the detail is created in Configuración when you click**, but
the change to the playbook's text, like everything, waits for **«Guardar
cambios»**. Your own details are informational: they do not add to the button's
number.

### The blanks the agent fills in

At the end, the Doctor lists separately the **text in square brackets** that
your agent completes when writing, like `[nombre si lo da]` (*[name if they
give it]*). It is not an error and there is nothing to fix: they are only
listed in case one of them should really be a variable. They do not count in
the button's number.

<Note>
**The «EN_MAYÚSCULAS» (*IN_CAPITALS*) placeholders are something else**, the
`«PRECIO_1»` kind (in capitals, with an underscore and inside angle quotes).
Those are counted by the «Faltan N datos por completar» notice, the index and
the **Por completar** panel, only in a funnel born from the Ecommerce template. In any funnel, **the
line that contains one of those placeholders does not reach your agent**: it is
left out entirely, without warning. If you see one in another playbook, replace
it with the real data or delete the sentence.
</Note>

## A playbook born from the Ecommerce template

If your funnel was born from the Ecommerce template (from the setup of a sales
funnel, or with **Cargar plantilla de Ecommerce**), the playbook brings a shared
block and three chapters — Saludo, En venta and Pedido confirmado (*Greeting,
Selling, Order confirmed*) — with **placeholders** (*datos por completar*) of the
`«PRODUCTO_MARCA»` kind where what belongs to your business goes. The editor
paints them amber, and above the document a notice says how many are missing:
*«Faltan N datos por completar: X en el bloque y Y en los capítulos»* (*N
details left to complete: X in the block and Y in the chapters*).

All of this also applies if in the setup you chose **Armar mi flujo desde 0**
(*Build my flow from scratch*): it brings the same block and the same three
chapters, but with the chapters' steps blank.

<Frame caption="A playbook from the template: the «Faltan 20 datos por completar» (20 details left to complete) notice at the top, the placeholders marked in the text and counted in the index, and the «Por completar» (To complete) button with the 20 in the bar.">
  <img src="/images/agente/02-manual-con-huecos.png" alt="Playbook of a funnel born from the Ecommerce template with the notice Faltan 20 datos por completar: 16 en el bloque y 4 en los capítulos (20 details left to complete: 16 in the block and 4 in the chapters) and the Ayúdame a completarlo (Help me complete it) button, placeholders such as «HORAS_SEGUIMIENTO_1» and «PRODUCTO_QUE_ES» marked in the text, each chapter's placeholders in the index, the Por completar (To complete) button with the 20 in the bar and the Recursos (Resources) panel open on the right" />
</Frame>

### The Por completar (To complete) panel

The **Por completar** button in the bar opens a panel with each placeholder once, in
the playbook's order: first the shared block and then each chapter.

- **Type the data and click «Completar en N sitios»** (*Complete in N places*): the
  placeholder changes everywhere it appears (the block counts once, even though
  the three chapters share it).
- **File placeholders** (`«ARCHIVO_PRECIOS»`, `«ARCHIVO_PRODUCTO»`…) are not
  typed: you pick a file from your **Library** and its mention stays. If you do
  not have it, **«No tengo este archivo»** (*I don't have this file*) removes it
  from the playbook.
- Nothing is written until you click **«Guardar cambios»**, like any other
  change in the editor.

### Letting the copilot help

**«Ayúdame a completarlo»** (*Help me complete it*), next to the placeholders
notice (or in the ⋯ menu of a chapter with placeholders), opens the copilot and
sends it the request in your name: you will see it as a message from you. The
copilot reads your playbook, first asks you for the product files, asks you one
question at a time and proposes the changes for you to approve. A change to the
block applies to the three chapters at once.

### When you can test and connect

- **Probar** turns on when chapter 1 and the **PRODUCTO** and **PRECIOS Y
  PROMOCIONES** sections of the block have no placeholders left; until then,
  hovering over the button tells you how many are missing. Chapters 2 and 3 can
  stay as template, and the notice at the top reminds you.
- **Conectar WhatsApp** (*Connect WhatsApp*) asks for three things: no
  placeholders, no chapter without steps and the payment, location and shipping
  details confirmed. While something is missing, the playbook and the
  **Canales** (*Channels*) screen show you **«Antes de conectar:»** (*Before
  connecting:*) with what is missing and a link to each item. To confirm, they
  show you those three sections as they ended up and ask you for a **«Sí, son
  correctos»** (*Yes, they are correct*).

<Note>
**A placeholder does not reach your customers.** Until you fill it in, your
agent does not receive it in its instructions: the line that contains it is
left out.
</Note>

### Loading the template into a funnel that already exists

On a computer, in a sales funnel with an empty playbook or with a single
chapter, the index (or the empty playbook) offers you **Cargar plantilla de
Ecommerce**, if the template is available for that funnel.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Fill in the three details">
    **Nombre del vendedor** (*Salesperson's name*), whether they introduce
    themselves as **Asesora** or **Asesor** (*advisor*, female or male), and
    the **Producto** (*Product*).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Click **Cargar plantilla** (*Load template*). The playbook is replaced by
    the template; the one you had stays in the
    [Historial](#the-history-rolling-back).
  </Step>
</Steps>

If that funnel's agent already serves a connected WhatsApp, the funnel has to be
paused first. If you click **Cargar plantilla** with the funnel active, the
template is not loaded and the window shows you **«Pausa el embudo en Canales
antes de cargar la plantilla.»** (*Pause the funnel in Channels before loading
the template.*), with the link **Ir a Canales** (*Go to Channels*).

## Testing the playbook

The **Probar** button in the bar opens, next to the document, a test chat with
your agent that looks like your customer's WhatsApp: your salesperson's photo
and name at the top, **«en línea»** (*online*) or **«escribiendo…»**
(*typing…*), and the bubbles over the wallpaper. You write as if you were the
customer and the agent answers with the **saved** playbook: it is the same
engine that serves WhatsApp, but nothing goes out over WhatsApp.

<Frame caption="The test phone next to the document: at the top, the chapter selector and the restart button; in the thread, the trace with the chapter and the tools the agent used.">
  <img src="/images/manual/04-probar.png" alt="WhatsApp-style test chat docked to the playbook: in the header, Sofía en línea (online), the 1 · Saludo (Greeting) selector and the restart button; in the thread, a customer asks for polo shirts in size M, the agent answers, below it the tools it used and the move to Capítulo 2 · En venta (Chapter 2 · On sale)" />
</Frame>

While the agent answers, the playbook moves with you: the chapter it is in
lights up and pulses while it thinks, and the PASO whose tool is running
breathes. Inside the thread you see the trace: the chapter it entered
(**«Capítulo N · nombre»** — *Chapter N · name*, or **«→ Capítulo N»** if it
advanced) and the tools it used. A click on the chapter in the trace takes you
to it. Under the phone, a line says which chapter it is in now.

- **Choosing where to start.** In the phone's header there is a selector with
  the chapters (**«N · nombre»**) and a **Reiniciar** (*Restart*) button (the
  circular arrow), which **deletes the test thread and opens it at that
  chapter**.
- **Probar desde aquí**, in the ⋯ menu of a chapter heading, does the same with
  that chapter.
- Closing and reopening **Probar** keeps the thread you had.
- If you have **unsaved changes**, the phone says so: *«Probando la versión
  guardada. Tienes cambios sin guardar.»* (*Testing the saved version. You have
  unsaved changes.*) The agent does not see them until you save. **Guardar y
  probar** (*Save and test*) saves and carries on.

<Note>
The test thread is real: it consumes balance like any conversation and your
agent uses its tools as it would with a customer. But it does not muddy your
numbers: it does not appear in the inbox or in **Clientes** (*Customers*), and
it does not count in **Resultados** (*Results*). And nothing goes out over
WhatsApp: the delivery stays inside the dashboard.
</Note>

**Probar** also works on the phone. It comes up grayed out with no funnel
chosen, in a funnel created with the old system (*«El chat de prueba solo está
disponible para embudos nuevos»* — *The test chat is only available for new
funnels*), if the playbook has no chapters or, in a template funnel, while there
are placeholders left in chapter 1 or in the block's PRODUCTO and PRECIOS Y
PROMOCIONES sections.

## Deleting a chapter

Choosing **«Eliminar capítulo»** in the ⋯ menu opens a confirmation with the
**«Eliminar capítulo»** (*Delete chapter*) button (or **«Eliminar sección»** —
*Delete section*). Before you decide, it warns you of up to four things, and
they are worth reading:

1. **«El borrado se aplica al guardar los cambios.»** (*The deletion is applied
   when you save the changes.*) Nothing has happened yet.
2. **How many mentions of that chapter will be left broken** — or, if there are
   none, *«Ninguna mención de capítulo quedará rota»* (*No chapter mention will
   be left broken*).
3. **How many § sections will be left without scope** and will go with it, if
   there are any.
4. **How many customers are sitting in that chapter today.** Sometimes it is
   counting them (*«Comprobando cuántos clientes están hoy en este capítulo…»*
   — *Checking how many customers are in this chapter today…*) and sometimes it
   cannot know, and then it asks you to check yourself: *«Si hay chats hoy en
   este capítulo, reasígnalos desde Clientes →»* (*If there are chats in this
   chapter today, reassign them from Clientes →*).

<Warning>
**That fourth warning is the real damage.** Customers left inside the deleted
chapter start being served with **the first chapter** until you reassign them:
your agent may greet them again as if they had just arrived, even if they had
already bought. The notice brings a link to **Clientes** (*Customers*) — it
opens in another tab, so you do not lose what you are editing — to reassign
them beforehand.
</Warning>

<Note>
**Chapters are not renumbered, and that is on purpose.** After deleting number
2 you are left with 1, 3, 4. The number **is the chapter's identity**: it is
used by the mentions, the jumps and the chapter each customer is sitting in.
Renumbering would leave those customers pointing at the wrong chapter. The
Doctor warns you about the gap, but it does not "fix" it by itself.

What is worth reviewing when you delete: who was naming that chapter. A mention
of a chapter that no longer exists is rejected, and that is where the
conversation stops.
</Note>

## Editing the playbook from Conversations

The full Playbook is not the only place. From the **Conversaciones**
(*Conversations*) inbox the **same** playbook opens in a side panel, and it also
**follows you**: it scrolls to the chapter that customer is in. It is the
convenient way to correct a sentence right when you see it sounds wrong.

There you can edit **lines and mentions**, and it saves by the same route. What
you **cannot** do from that panel is **create, rename or delete chapters**:
that lives only in the full Playbook screen («Abrir el manual completo» — *Open
the full playbook*).

<Note>
**The color of the chapter chip** — the one you see in Clientes and in the
conversation list — is not changed from the Playbook: it is changed from
**Conversaciones**, and that change is saved without spending one of your ten
Historial versions.
</Note>

## From the phone

With the playbook open on a phone the document is **read-only**. The index on
the left does not appear, and the bar keeps what works there: **Cambios
medidos** (icon only), **Datos**, **Probar** and the save button, plus undo,
redo and **Descartar cambios** when you have unsaved changes.

At the top a band comes up: *«En el teléfono puedes retocar datos: precios,
teléfonos y enlaces, en todas las veces que aparezcan…»* (*On the phone you can
touch up data: prices, phone numbers and links, everywhere they appear…*), with
two buttons.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tap «Retocar datos» (Touch up data)">
    The list of your playbook's prices, phone numbers and links opens, with how
    many times each one appears.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap the piece of data">
    Write the new value, choose which appearances to apply it to (all of them
    come ticked) and tap **«Actualizar N»** (*Update N*).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Tap **«Guardar cambios»** in the bar. It spends **just one** of your ten
    Historial versions, even if you change several pieces of data.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If the list comes up empty, it is because it found no prices or phone numbers:
a price is detected when it has its currency symbol in front, like «S/ 89».

Also, from the phone you can **Probar** your agent and **roll back a change**
from **Cambios medidos**.

To **rewrite the script** — create chapters, change the steps, move sections —
or **fill in the template placeholders** you need a computer. The
**«Enviármelo»** (*Send it to me*) button (in the empty playbook, **«💻
Enviármelo a la computadora»**) is there for exactly that: it opens your phone's
share menu, or copies the link, so you can carry on on the big screen.

## If something does not work

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="I changed the playbook and my agent still says the old thing">
    Look at the indicator in the bar: if it says **«Cambios sin guardar»**, your
    agent does not have them yet. Your agent (and **Probar** too) uses the
    **saved** playbook. Click **Guardar cambios** and test again.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The conversation does not move on to the next chapter">
    The first thing to check: an advance written in prose («pasa al siguiente»
    — *go to the next one*). Open the **Doctor**: if it says **«El capítulo N no
    avanza a ningún capítulo»**, in the final step type `@` → **Capítulos** and
    choose the destination. Also check that the destination chapter exists. See
    [Making the conversation advance](#making-the-conversation-advance).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The file does not reach the customer">
    Hover over the file mention: if its dot is amber it is **paused**; if it is
    red, it **does not exist**. If you wrote the marker by hand, check the `#`
    signs and the nickname. The rest is in
    [The file Library](/en/guides/library).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="My agent says «(pendiente)» instead of my Yape">
    The business detail has no value, or no longer exists in Configuración (for
    example, because someone deleted it). Write it, or create it again, in
    **Configuración** → **«Datos del negocio»** and save. The Doctor warns you
    about both cases: about the detail without a value, with **«… no tiene valor
    en Ajustes»** (*… has no value in Settings*).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The notice to a third party does not arrive">
    Open the **Doctor**. If the notice goes to a number, it must carry the
    country code and be written all together, with no spaces in the middle (for
    example 51987654321; a `+` in front does no harm). If it goes by name, that
    name must be in your **Números frecuentes** (or be **Equipo de ventas**)
    with its number.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="On saving it tells me a section «se quedó sin contenido» (was left empty)">
    You emptied a shared section that applies to chapters, and saving it like
    that would erase that context. Undo with `⌘Z`, write something inside or
    delete it from its **⋯** menu.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I came back and my unsaved changes are gone">
    The local copy lives in the browser where you wrote, lasts 7 days and is
    discarded if the playbook changed on the server in the meantime. When that
    last thing happens, the dashboard warns you: *«El manual cambió en el
    servidor desde tus cambios sin guardar — se descartó el borrador local.»*
    (*The playbook changed on the server since your unsaved changes — the local
    draft was discarded.*)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="«Probar» is grayed out">
    Hover over the button: it tells you why. The causes are not having a funnel
    chosen, a funnel from the old system, a playbook with no chapters or, in a
    template funnel, unfilled placeholders in chapter 1 or in PRODUCTO and
    PRECIOS Y PROMOCIONES.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="In «Cambios medidos» it won't let me roll back">
    Expand the row: it tells you the reason. If it says you made other changes
    afterwards, use the [Historial](#the-history-rolling-back) to choose what to
    recover.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What each thing is called outside the editor

This table is **the place in this guide** where the technical names come
together. It is useful if you handle the playbook from your assistant over the
bridge, or if you run into them in the dashboard: they peek out in the **«Ver
como lo recibe el agente»** window and in the chapter's small execution window.

| What you see in the editor | What it is called in the assistant connection | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The chapter's **name**, above each block | `chapter_label` | The title. It goes without a number. |
| The number: **«Capítulo 3»** and the index number | `display_order` | The chapter's number in the funnel. It is the one written in the advance lines. |
| The **chapter's lines** (the editor puts no label on them) | `thought_chain` | The script: what your agent executes step by step. |
| **«Tu papel aquí:»** inside each chapter | `role` | Who your agent is in that chapter. |
| The **shared section §** and its scope | `context` | The knowledge and the rules that apply to several chapters. |
| The **«se ejecuta 1 vez»** chip and the **«Se ejecuta una sola vez»** checkbox | `execute_once` | That the chapter is said once and moves on. |
| The **«al terminar pasa a»** list | `advance_to` | Which chapter it jumps to on finishing. |
| *(not in the editor)* | `chapter_index` | The key your assistant uses to edit that chapter. You do not assign it. |
| *(no longer exists)* | `examples` | Conversation examples. See the warning below. |

<Warning>
**Conversation examples no longer exist, and saving deletes them.** If your
playbook came with examples from an earlier era, the editor **does not show
them** and, every time you save a chapter, that field is written empty. It is
worth knowing before you go looking for them.
</Warning>

<Note>
**The same three names change depending on where you look.** In the editor they
carry no label (except «Tu papel aquí:»). In **«Ver como lo recibe el agente»**
they come out already translated: *role — quién es* (who it is),
*thought_chain — qué hace* (what it does), *context — conocimiento y límites*
(knowledge and limits). And in the **Historial** and in the copilot's proposals
they are **Rol**, **Cadena de pensamiento** and **Contexto**. It is the same
piece three times.

And if your assistant gives you back some English name you do not recognize, it
is not broken: the system understands the names in English and in Spanish.
</Note>

### In what order the chapter reaches your agent

First the **persona**, the **customer's data**, their **notes** and their
**appointments**. Then the chapter: its **name**, who it is (**ROL:**), the
script (**CADENA DE PENSAMIENTO:**, with an added instruction after it), the
**context** — which brings inside it its own labels **CONTEXTO:** and **PUNTOS
CLAVE:** —, the chapter's **tools** and **files**. Between the name and the
role two system notices may slip in: which PASO it enters from, and whether the
playbook is empty.

Each part **only appears if it has content**.

## From your assistant (optional)

If you have connected Claude, ChatGPT or Codex over
[the bridge](/en/reference/connection-url), you can read and change the
playbook by talking. **Reading** the playbook and its history comes switched on
out of the box; **saving** does not — it is a destructive write — and it has to
be asked for in the URL you connect with. The URL the dashboard gives you
already includes it:

```
https://mcp.darkfunnels.ai/mcp?features=all
```

Four pieces of advice that avoid the most expensive messes:

<Warning>
**Don't let anyone "normalize" your playbook.** An assistant that rewrites
whole chapters "to make them consistent" tramples text that was working. Ask
for surgical changes: the chapter we discussed, the part we discussed. And ask
it to show you the proposal **before** saving.
</Warning>

- **One single save for all the changes.** Each save burns one of your ten
  versions, whether it comes from the dashboard or from your assistant.
- **After creating a chapter, have it reread the playbook** and use the real
  number that came out. With gaps in the numbering — normal after deleting one —
  the number it assumes may clash with that of a live chapter, and the advance
  lines point by number.
- **Deleting a chapter shifts the internal keys of the ones after it.**
  Creating does not. If you see your assistant editing "from memory" twice in a
  row without rereading, stop it.

Whatever **the copilot** proposes, on the other hand, is reviewed on this same
screen: see [What the copilot proposes to you](#what-the-copilot-proposes-to-you).

## A pixel without a website

The classic pixel is a piece of code that lives on a website and tells Meta
every time someone buys. In a WhatsApp funnel there is no website and no
browser, so that code doesn't exist. What there is, is this: when a customer
matches one of your rules (they reach a chapter of the sales playbook, or your
agent records a sale), DarkFunnels sends Meta a notification (Meta calls it an
“event”) through its **Conversions API**, the channel Meta offers for
notifications that don't come from a website.

Along with the notification travel the data Meta needs to recognize the person:
the phone number, the first name, the last name and the country, first
converted into a **fingerprint** that cannot be reversed. Meta doesn't receive
that data in the clear. What does **not** travel is the conversation.

What it's good for:

- Meta can optimize your campaigns for purchases and not only for
  conversations started, when it enables that objective on your account
  (further down we tell you what that depends on).
- You can normally build audiences of real buyers and lookalike audiences
  based on them.
- In Ads Manager you see purchases next to each ad, not just chats.

## Before you start: the three things to know

<Warning>
**1. You need to be an admin or a developer on the business portfolio that owns
the pixel.** The business portfolio is Meta's company account (the old Business
Manager); today those permissions are called **Full control** and **Apps and
integrations**. Without one of the two, the **Generate access token** button
appears greyed out with the notice *“A prerequisite is not met”*. It's the most
common blocker, and DarkFunnels can't fix it: whoever administers the portfolio
grants it to you, in **Business settings → Users → People**.

**2. What you paste is the ID of a pixel or dataset, not that of an app.** In
Events Manager they appear mixed together. If you open a Facebook app you won't
find the token button: this dashboard's notifications go to the pixel's
dataset.

**3. The sales that reach Meta are the ones your agent records.** The ones you
enter yourself in the **Conversiones** (*Conversions*) card of the customer's
profile, from **Clientes** (*Customers*) or from **Conversaciones**
(*Conversations*), are not sent, even if you use **Registrar conversión**
(*Record conversion*) to have the AI fill in the form for you. It's on purpose:
the pixel exists so Meta learns from the sales your agent closed in the
conversation.
</Warning>

So far Meta knows you exist. You still haven't told it **when** to notify it:
that's what the rules are for, and **without rules nothing is sent, except the
test**.

## The rules: when each event is sent

Each rule is a sentence: *when a customer in this funnel does such a thing, Meta
receives such an event*. For almost every business two are enough: **records a
sale → Purchase** and **the chapter where the customer has already shown
interest → Lead**.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open «Nueva regla» (New rule)">
    On the pixel's card, next to **Reglas** (*Rules*), click **Añadir regla**
    (*Add rule*). The window opens set up for the most common case: **Registra
    una venta** (*Records a sale*) with **Compra (Purchase)**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the funnel">
    In **Embudo** (*Funnel*), choose the funnel whose customers count for this
    rule. If you only have one, it's already selected. Each rule names a single funnel:
    if you sell with two, create a rule for each one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say what has to happen">
    In **Cuando el cliente** (*When the customer*), leave **Registra una
    venta** or choose another option from the table below. With **Llega a un
    capítulo** (*Reaches a chapter*), **Capítulo** (*Chapter*) appears, with
    the chapters of that funnel's playbook.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the event and save">
    In **Evento que recibe Meta** (*Event Meta receives*) the usual event for
    that case is already filled in. You can swap it for another one from the
    list or for a **Personalizado…** (*Custom…*) one with your own name
    (letters, numbers and underscores, starting with a letter). The full
    sentence is assembled below, for example *«Cuando un cliente de «Moda Sol»
    compra, Meta recibe Compra (Purchase).»* (*When a customer of “Moda Sol”
    buys, Meta receives Compra (Purchase).*) Read it and click **Guardar
    regla** (*Save rule*).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="The «Nueva regla» (New rule) window: funnel, when and event, and below, the rule written out as a sentence before you save it.">
  <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/23-df-nueva-regla.png" alt="«Nueva regla» (New rule) window with the Embudo (Funnel), Cuando el cliente (When the customer) and Evento que recibe Meta (Event Meta receives) fields, and the rule's sentence" />
</Frame>

| When the customer… | Suggested event | What it tells Meta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Llega a un capítulo** (*Reaches a chapter*) | Cliente potencial (`Lead`) | Someone advanced to that point in the playbook. Here **Compra is not offered**: reaching a chapter doesn't say how much was sold. |
| **Registra una venta** (*Records a sale*) | Compra (`Purchase`) | Someone bought. It goes with the amount and the currency your agent recorded; if the sale is missing either of the two, it goes without an amount. |
| **Agenda una cita** (*Books an appointment*) | Cita agendada (`Schedule`) | Someone booked an appointment. |
| **Deja sus datos (lead)** (*Leaves their details (lead)*) | Cliente potencial (`Lead`) | Someone left their contact details. |
| **Contrata un servicio** (*Signs up for a service*) | Compra (`Purchase`) | Someone signed up for a service. If the conversion carries an amount, it goes with it. |
| **Cualquier conversión** (*Any conversion*) | Compra (`Purchase`) | Any sale, appointment, lead or service your agent records (reaching a chapter doesn't count). Careful: with this rule an appointment or a lead reaches Meta as a purchase, and if they carry no amount, as a purchase with no amount. |

The details worth knowing:

- **Chapter 1 can't be chosen.** Every contact is born there. Rules start at 2.
- **The rule points at the chapter number, not at its name.** If you reorder or
  delete chapters in the playbook, the rule keeps pointing at the same number:
  the dashboard reminds you of this when you create it, and it's worth
  reviewing your rules after touching the playbook.
- **A jump skips the ones in between.** If your agent moves the customer from
  chapter 2 to 4, the rule for 3 doesn't fire.
- **Each arrival is sent once.** The system records what it has already
  delivered and doesn't repeat it. If the customer leaves the chapter and comes
  back in later, that's another arrival and another notification goes out.
- **If two rules on the same pixel match the same occurrence, the one you
  created first wins.** If you have two pixels, each one sends its own
  notification.
- **Moving the customer between chapters by hand also counts.** If you drag a
  customer in the **Capítulos** (*Chapters*) view of Clientes or change their
  chapter from their profile, the rule fires just as if your agent had done it.
- **A rule can't be edited.** To change it, remove it with the wastebasket icon
  on its row and create the new one.
- **Each pixel allows up to 50 rules.**
- **Voiding a sale doesn't subtract anything.** The Conversions API has no
  refund notification and doesn't accept subtracting a purchase: the Purchase
  already sent stays.

<Note>
Pausing the pixel with its toggle stops sending your rules' notifications,
without deleting rules or history (the **Probar conexión** test keeps working).
What happens while it's paused is not sent afterwards: when you switch it back
on, only new occurrences go out.
</Note>

### If you have an old «chapter → Compra» rule

It used to be possible to send **Compra** (*Purchase*) on reaching a chapter.
Today the dashboard no longer allows it, because that purchase reaches Meta
**without the amount** of the sale. If you type «Purchase» as a custom event in
a chapter rule, the dashboard rejects it with *«Compra necesita el monto de la
venta…»* (*Purchase needs the sale amount…*).

If your pixel already had a rule like that, it keeps working (and sending
purchases with no amount) until you delete it. You'll see it with this notice
in red underneath:

<Warning>
*«Esta regla manda Compra sin el monto de la venta. Primero bórrala y después
crea «Registra una venta» → «Compra (Purchase)»: con las dos a la vez, Meta
contaría dos compras.»* (*This rule sends Purchase without the sale amount. First
delete it and then create “Records a sale” → “Purchase”: with both at once, Meta
would count two purchases.*)
</Warning>

**Do it in that order.** If you try to create the sale rule first (or any other
one that sends Compra on recording a conversion in that same funnel), the
dashboard won't let you and warns you: *«Este embudo ya manda Compra al llegar
a un capítulo. Borra esa regla antes de crear Compra al registrar una venta, o
Meta contará dos compras.»* (*This funnel already sends Purchase on reaching a
chapter. Delete that rule before creating Purchase on recording a sale, or Meta
will count two purchases.*)

## What each send tells you

Two things on the tab tell you whether the pixel is working: the **Cobertura
del píxel** (*Pixel coverage*) line, at the top, and **Últimos envíos**
(*Recent sends*), on each pixel. You don't have to ask anyone for a technical
log.

### Pixel coverage

As soon as you have a pixel, the **Manda tus ventas reales a Meta** card adds a
line like this one:

*Cobertura del píxel: 8 de 10 ventas de los últimos 7 días llegaron a Meta con
su monto.* (*Pixel coverage: 8 of 10 sales from the last 7 days reached Meta
with their amount.*)

It counts the sales your agent recorded in the last 7 days, across all your
funnels, and how many of them reached Meta as a **Purchase** with their amount.
If your agent didn't sell that week, it tells you so: *«tu agente no registró
ventas en los últimos 7 días»* (*your agent didn't record any sales in the last
7 days*). If you see a dash (—), it couldn't be calculated at that moment: open
the tab again later.

If the two numbers don't match, the difference is usually in one of these
cases:

- **The funnel of those sales has no sale rule.** That funnel needs **Registra
  una venta → Compra (Purchase)** (or **Cualquier conversión → Compra**).
- **The pixel was paused, or didn't exist yet, when the sale was made.** Those
  sales count in the total, but they weren't sent and won't be. The figure
  catches up on its own as the days go by.
- **The send came out Omitido (Skipped) or Falló (Failed).** The reason is in
  **Últimos envíos**.
- **The sale rule sends another event** (a custom one, for example): it reaches
  Meta, but this line only counts Purchases.
- **The Purchase went out through an old chapter rule.** That one carries no
  amount and doesn't count.
- **The sale was recorded without an amount or without a currency.** It reaches
  Meta as a Purchase, but without an amount, and this line doesn't count it.

### Recent sends

Click **Últimos envíos** on the pixel's card: you'll see the last 20
notifications, one row each with its status, the event, where it came from
(**capítulo** (*chapter*), **conversión** (*conversion*) or **prueba**
(*test*)), the reason if something went wrong, and the time. To close it,
**Ocultar envíos** (*Hide sends*).

| Status | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Enviado** (*Sent*) | Meta replied that it received it (*Meta lo recibió*, *Meta received it*). | Nothing. |
| **Falló** (*Failed*) | Meta rejected it, didn't reply, or the pixel has no token (*No hay token guardado para este píxel.*, *There's no token saved for this pixel.*). The reason appears in the row. | If it mentions the token (“OAuth”, invalid, revoked) or says there's no token, generate another one in Events Manager, delete the pixel and create it again. If it mentions the network or that there was no response, it's usually a momentary outage: that notification is not repeated; once the outage is over, the next one goes out normally. |
| **Omitido** (*Skipped*) | The rule matched, but the notification wasn't sent: *El contacto no tiene teléfono real ni clic de anuncio: Meta no podría emparejarlo.* (*The contact has no real phone number and no ad click: Meta couldn't match them.*), or *El evento tenía más de 7 días; Meta no acepta eventos tan antiguos.* (*The event was more than 7 days old; Meta doesn't accept events that old.*) | Nothing to fix on that contact. If it's almost all of them, read [Common problems](/en/guides/meta-pixel#common-problems). |

Failures with Meta **are not retried automatically**: a revoked token is not
going to start working again by insisting. That's why the error stays visible
here instead of spinning silently.

On Meta's side, the activity shows up in the dataset's **Overview** tab, and
Meta warns it can take up to 30 minutes to show it.

## The ad click

When a customer messages you from a **“Send WhatsApp message”** ad, their
message usually carries, hidden, the ad they came from and a click identifier.
We store it alongside the contact without you having to do anything, and if the
same customer comes back through another ad, the latest one is kept. You won't
see the click identifier on their profile: the send to Meta uses it.

With that there are two ways of telling Meta what happened:

- **By phone number**, the usual one. Meta receives the fingerprint of the
  number and matches it to the person; if that person saw or tapped your ads,
  it attributes it with its usual rules. The customer needs to write with a
  visible number and with a recognized country. If they arrive with a hidden
  number and didn't come from an ad either, the notification is skipped. If
  they came from an ad, it goes out anyway, but without the WhatsApp Business
  account ID (the one in the next point) the click doesn't travel: Meta only
  receives the fingerprints of the first name, the last name and the country,
  and an internal identifier, so it's hard for Meta to match it.
- **By the exact click**, if, when creating the pixel, you also save the
  **WhatsApp Business account ID**. That ID belongs to numbers registered on
  the WhatsApp Business Platform (Meta's official API), and it's visible in
  **Business settings → Accounts → WhatsApp accounts**.

**In DarkFunnels your number is connected by scanning a QR code or with a code,
not through that API.** That's why you normally won't have that ID: leave it
empty and the notifications will go by phone number, as explained above. If
your number is also on that platform and you save the ID, the card shows the
**Atribución por clic** (*Click attribution*) badge: the notification for a
customer who arrived from an ad goes out marked as a messaging conversion and
Meta hangs it off the specific ad; the notification for everyone else goes by
phone number. In that mode, `Lead` travels with the name Meta uses for
messaging, and Meta understands a shorter list of events there: an appointment
or a custom event may not count.

The ad each customer came from is also used by the **Anuncios** (*Ads*) page,
which brings together what you spend on Meta with what happened in your chats.
It's covered in [Connect your Meta ads](/en/guides/meta-ads).

## In Ads Manager

For purchases to appear in the campaign results, the ad has to read from the
same dataset. Edit the click-to-WhatsApp ad and scroll down to the **Tracking**
block, below the welcome template: tick **Website events** and check that the
dataset shown is the one you connected here. Click **Publish**.

</Note>

## Common problems

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="In “Connect data” I chose “Messaging” and Meta says my Page doesn't meet the requirements">
    That route is for numbers that are on the WhatsApp Business Platform. With
    your number connected by QR, Meta normally won't find an eligible Page. Go
    back and choose **Web**: the classic pixel receives this dashboard's
    notifications just the same.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="«Probar conexión» (Test connection) says Meta received it, but I see nothing in Meta">
    An **Enviado** (*Sent*) row in **Últimos envíos** (*Recent sends*) means
    Meta replied that it received it. The test travels with your test code, so
    it shows up in the dataset's **Test events** tab, and that code has to
    belong to that same dataset. Meta asks for that tab to stay open during the
    test: open it before clicking **Probar conexión** and don't close it.

    If it doesn't appear, compare the ID: on the pixel's card you see its last
    four digits, and they have to match those of the dataset you're looking at
    in Meta. Real notifications, on the other hand, show up in the **Overview**
    tab, and Meta warns it can take up to 30 minutes to show them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="«Meta rechazó el evento» (Meta rejected the event): “Invalid OAuth access token”">
    The token belongs to another pixel, it was pasted incomplete or someone
    revoked it in Events Manager. Generate a new one, delete the pixel from the
    dashboard and create it with the good token. Write down the rules first:
    deleting erases them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A customer bought and there's no send at all">
    Check in this order:

    - The pixel already existed and was **active** (toggle on) when the sale
      was recorded. What happened before you created it or while it was paused
      is not sent afterwards.
    - There's a sale rule **for that funnel** (each rule names its funnel).
    - The sale was recorded by **your agent** in the conversation, not entered
      by you on the customer's profile.
    - It isn't the sale the system records on its own when an order is
      charged.

    If it all checks out, the send is in **Últimos envíos** with its status
    (the list shows the 20 most recent). The **Cobertura del píxel** (*Pixel
    coverage*) line tells you at a glance how many of the week's sales got
    through.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="It won't let me save the sale rule: «Este embudo ya manda Compra al llegar a un capítulo…»">
    That funnel has an old rule that sends Compra (*Purchase*) on reaching a
    chapter, and with both Meta would count each purchase twice. First delete
    the chapter rule (it's the one with the notice in red) and then create
    **Registra una venta → Compra (Purchase)**.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Almost everything comes out «Omitido» (Skipped)">
    The reason is in each row. If it says the contact has no real phone number
    and no ad click, these are customers who wrote with their number hidden
    (WhatsApp lets you hide it; in your inbox they show up with their name but
    no flag or number or, if they have no name, as «Contacto» (*Contact*)
    followed by four digits) or with no recognized country, and who didn't
    arrive from an ad: there is nothing Meta could match and they are skipped
    on purpose. If it says the event was more than 7 days old, the send was
    delayed (for example, because the system was down) and the occurrence was
    already old: Meta doesn't accept anything more than 7 days old, so it isn't
    sent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I want to change the token, the name or a rule">
    The dashboard doesn't let you edit the token, the name or the WhatsApp
    Business account ID: delete the pixel (wastebasket icon, **Eliminar**
    (*Delete*)) and create it again with the new information. You lose its
    rules and its send history, not your contacts' click information. A rule
    can't be edited either: remove it and create the new one.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I don't want to send anything yet">
    Create the pixel anyway and leave it with no rules, or switch it off with
    the toggle. In both cases nothing is sent to Meta, except the test if you
    click **Probar conexión**.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What the pixel doesn't do

So you don't get a surprise:

- It doesn't send the sales you enter yourself on the customer's profile (from
  **Clientes** or **Conversaciones**), even if the AI filled in the form for
  you. Nor the sale the system records on its own when an order your agent
  didn't record in the chat is charged: for Meta, what counts is what the agent
  recorded.
- It doesn't subtract anything when a sale is voided.
- It doesn't send notifications more than 7 days old, nor while the pixel is
  paused or has no rules.
- It doesn't pass Meta the content of the conversations. What does travel: the
  fingerprints of the phone number, the first name and the last name, the
  country and an internal contact identifier; on conversions, the amount, the
  currency, the product code and the order identifier, when there are any;
  and, if you use click attribution, the click identifier and the one for your
  WhatsApp Business account, which are Meta's data, not yours.
- It doesn't move money and doesn't touch your campaigns or your ads: it only
  sends notifications to Meta.

## And now

Message your agent from another phone (with the number visible, and with the
test code already removed if you don't want it to come out as a test), let it
take you to your rule's chapter and look at **Últimos envíos**: that's the
honest test. When your agent starts selling, the **Cobertura del píxel** line
will tell you how many of those sales reached Meta. If the ad reads from that
dataset, in a few hours the real purchases start appearing next to your ads.

This is what closes the loop with your ads. Up to here your agent sells over
WhatsApp; from here on Meta finds out who actually bought, and with that list it
refines who it shows your ads to.

## A pixel without a website

The classic pixel is a piece of code that lives on a website and tells Meta
every time someone buys. In a WhatsApp funnel there is no website and no
browser, so that code doesn't exist. What there is, is this: when a customer
matches one of your rules — reaching a chapter of the sales playbook, or your
agent recording a sale — DarkFunnels sends Meta a notification (Meta calls it
an “event”) through its **Conversions API**, the channel Meta offers for
notifications that don't come from a website.

Along with the notification travel the data Meta needs to recognize the person:
the phone number, the name and the country, always converted into a
**fingerprint** that cannot be reversed (Meta never receives the data in the
clear). What does **not** travel is the conversation.

What it's good for, in three sentences: Meta can optimize your campaigns for
purchases and not only for conversations started, when it enables that objective
on your account (further down we tell you when); you can normally build
audiences of real buyers and lookalike audiences from them; and in Ads Manager
you see purchases next to each ad, not just chats.

## Before you start: the three things to know

<Warning>
**1. You need to be an admin or a developer on the business portfolio that owns
the pixel.** The business portfolio is Meta's company account (the old Business
Manager); today those permissions are called **Full control** and **Apps and
integrations**. Without one of the two, the **Generate access token** button
appears greyed out with the notice *“A prerequisite is not met”*. It's the most
common blocker, and DarkFunnels can't fix it: whoever administers the portfolio
grants it to you, in **Business settings → Users → People**.

**2. What you paste is the ID of a pixel or dataset, not that of an app.** In
Events Manager they appear mixed together. If you open a Facebook app you won't
find the token button: this dashboard's notifications go to the pixel's dataset.

**3. Only the sales your agent records count.** The ones you enter by hand from
**Customers** are not sent, on purpose: the pixel exists so Meta learns from the
sales that came out of a conversation.
</Warning>

## What you need to get from Events Manager

Three pieces of information, all three from the same place: Meta's **Events
Manager** (at `business.facebook.com/events_manager`). If your ad account
already has a pixel, skip to step 2.

To get there, from **Ads Manager** hover over the icon bar on the left: the menu
expands with the names and **Events Manager** is at the bottom.

The screenshots are from the Spanish interface: the DarkFunnels dashboard is in
Spanish, and Meta's own screens appear in whatever language your Meta account
uses.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the dataset, if you don't have one">
    In the Events Manager left-hand panel, click **Connect data**, the first
    option, with the plus sign. (Your existing datasets are under **Datasets**;
    in older accounts, **Data sources**.)

    <Frame caption="The Events Manager left-hand panel: “Connect data” is the first option.">
      <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/02-em-menu-conectar-datos.png" alt="Events Manager side panel with the Connect data option" />
    </Frame>

    Only then does the window with the five options appear. Choose **Web**:
    that's what creates the classic pixel, and it's the one that works for a
    WhatsApp funnel.

    <Frame caption="“Connect data” offers Web, App, Offline, CRM and Messaging. For a number connected by QR, the option is Web.">
      <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/00-meta-conectar-datos.png" alt="Events Manager “Connect a new data source” dialog" />
    </Frame>

    Give it a name, leave the Conversions API checkbox ticked and click
    **Create**. Meta asks which ad accounts to connect (tick the ones that
    advertise your WhatsApp) and then how you want to send the data: choose
    **Set up the Conversions API** and, when it proposes a partner, **See other
    setup options → Set up manually**. You don't need to follow the “manual
    implementation” wizard that opens afterwards: close it, the dataset already
    exists.

    <Frame caption="Meta proposes a paid partner; “See other options” reveals “Set up manually”, which is the route with no extra code.">
      <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/04-meta-api-otras-opciones.png" alt="Conversions API setup options, with “Set up manually” at the end" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the pixel ID">
    In **Datasets** → your pixel → **Settings** tab. The ID is the long number,
    around 16 digits, that appears as **Dataset ID** (and under the name in the
    list on the left).

    <Frame caption="The Settings tab shows the ID that goes into DarkFunnels.">
      <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/07-meta-configuracion-identificador.png" alt="Dataset Settings tab with the ID" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate the access token">
    In the same tab, scroll down to **Conversions API** → **Set up direct
    integration** → tick **Set up without Dataset Quality API** → **Generate
    access token**.

    <Frame caption="The “Generate access token” button. If it's greyed out, you're missing the permission on the business portfolio.">
      <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/08-meta-integracion-directa-generar-token.png" alt="“Set up direct integration” block with the Generate access token button" />
    </Frame>

    Copy the whole thing: it's long, it usually starts with `EAA`, and Meta
    warns that it **does not store it**: if you close the page without copying

      <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/09-meta-token-generado.png" alt="Generated access token, with the token text hidden" />

  <Step title="Copy the test code">
    **Test events** tab → under **Select channel** choose **Website** → expand
    **Confirm that your server events are set up correctly** and copy the

    </Frame>

<Tip>
Have the three pieces of information at hand before you open the dashboard. The
token is stored as a fingerprint and can't be looked up afterwards: if you paste
it wrong, the way out is to delete the pixel and create it again.
</Tip>

## Connect it in DarkFunnels

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the pixels dashboard">
    In the menu: **Customize → Pixels** → **Add pixel**. The card at the top,
    **Where do I get this information?**, summarizes this guide in case you need
    it without leaving the dashboard.

    <Frame caption="The Pixels tab: the collapsed help at the top and, below it, each pixel with its rules and its sends.">
      <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/20-df-panel-pixeles.png" alt="Customize → Pixels dashboard with one connected pixel and two rules" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the form">
    A **Name** you'll recognize (“Peru campaigns”), the **Pixel ID (dataset)**
    and the **Conversions API access token**. The **Test code** goes in its own
    box. Leave the **WhatsApp Business account ID** empty if you connected your
    number by scanning the QR (further down we tell you when you should fill it
    in).

    <Frame caption="The creation form. The token is stored encrypted and is not shown again.">
      <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/22-df-formulario-nuevo-pixel.png" alt="“New Meta pixel” form with its five fields" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and test the connection">
    **Save pixel** leaves the card with the **Token saved** badge. Click **Test
    connection**: if you didn't save the test code, it asks for it in a box
    right there (paste it and click **Test**; that code is used only for that
    test and is not stored). If Meta accepts, you'll see the notice *“Meta
    received the test event. See it in Events Manager → Test events.”* and a
    **Sent · Lead · test** row under **Recent sends**.

    <Frame caption="“Test connection” asks for the code if it isn't saved; every attempt is recorded in “Recent sends”.">
      <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/24-df-ultimos-envios-y-probar.png" alt="Sends block with the test code box and two test sends received" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Remove the test code when you're done">
    While the code is saved, real notifications also go out marked as tests:
    Meta counts them all the same, but they show up in **Test events** mixed in
    with your tests. The card flags this with the **Test mode** badge; **Remove
    test code** takes it out without touching anything else.
  </Step>
</Steps>

So far Meta knows you exist. You still haven't told it **when** to notify it:
that's what the rules are for, and without them nothing is sent.

## The rules: when each event is sent

Each rule is a sentence: *when a customer in this funnel does such a thing, Meta
receives such an event*. You write it with **Add rule**:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the funnel">
    In **Funnel**, the agent whose customers count for this rule. Each rule
    names a single funnel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say what has to happen">
    In **When the customer**, choose between reaching a chapter or recording a
    conversion. If it's a chapter, choose which one in **Chapter**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Leave the suggested event or change it">
    In **Event Meta receives** the usual event for that case is already filled
    in. The full sentence is assembled below: read it and click **Save rule**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="The rule reads as a whole before you save it: funnel, chapter and event.">
  <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/23-df-nueva-regla.png" alt="“New rule” editor with the sentence describing the rule" />
</Frame>

| When the customer… | Suggested event | What it tells Meta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reaches a chapter** | `Lead` | Someone advanced to that point in the sales playbook. |
| **Records a sale** | `Purchase` | Someone bought, with amount and currency: Meta requires them on a purchase. |
| **Books an appointment** | `Schedule` | Someone booked, through the chat or from your booking page. |
| **Leaves their details (lead)** | `Lead` | Someone left a name and contact details. |
| **Signs up for a service** | `Purchase` | Same as a sale. |
| **Any conversion** | `Purchase` | Any of the above. Careful: with this rule an appointment or a lead reaches Meta as a purchase. |

The suggested event can be swapped for another one from the catalog or for a
**custom** one with your own name. For almost every business two rules are
enough: **the chapter where the customer has already shown interest → Lead** and
**records a sale → Purchase**.

The details worth knowing:

- **Chapter 1 can't be chosen.** Every contact is born there, so nobody
  “reaches” it. Rules start at 2.
- **The rule points at the chapter number, not at its name.** If you reorder or
  delete chapters in the sales playbook, the rule keeps pointing at the same
  number: the dashboard reminds you of this when you create it, and it's worth
  reviewing your rules after touching the playbook.
- **A jump skips the ones in between.** If your agent moves the customer from
  chapter 2 to 4, the rule for 3 doesn't fire.
- **Each arrival is sent once.** The system records what it has already
  delivered and doesn't repeat it. If the customer leaves the chapter and comes
  back in later, that's another arrival and another notification goes out. If
  two rules on the same pixel match the same occurrence, the first one you wrote
  wins.
- **Moving the customer between chapters by hand also counts.** If you drag a
  customer in the funnel or change their chapter from their profile, the rule
  fires just as if your agent had done it.
- **Voiding a sale doesn't subtract anything.** The Conversions API has no
  refund notification and doesn't accept subtracting a purchase: the `Purchase`
  already sent stays.
- **With click attribution, Meta understands a short list of events** (purchase,
  lead, cart, order…). We translate `Lead` into the name Meta expects there; an
  appointment or an event with its own name may not count in that mode.

<Note>
Pausing the pixel with its toggle stops sending everything, without deleting
rules or history. What happens while it's paused is not sent afterwards: when
you switch it back on, only new occurrences go out.
</Note>

## What each send tells you

**Recent sends**, on the pixel card, is the proof of life: the last 20
notifications, one row each with its outcome. You don't have to ask anyone for a
technical log: here you see what happened to each one.

| Status | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sent** | Meta received it. | Nothing. |
| **Failed** | Meta rejected it or it couldn't be reached. The reason appears in the row. | If it mentions the token (“OAuth”, invalid, revoked), generate another one in Events Manager, delete the pixel and create it again. If it says there was no response, it was a momentary outage: that notification is not repeated, but the next one will go out normally. |
| **Skipped** | The rule matched, but there was nothing Meta could match on: the contact had no usable phone number (hidden number or no recognized country) and didn't come from an ad, or the occurrence was more than 7 days old. | Nothing to fix on that contact. If it's all of them, read “Common problems”. |

Failures with Meta **are not retried automatically**: a revoked token is not
going to start working again by insisting. That's why the error stays visible
here instead of spinning silently.

On Meta's side, the **Test events** tab only gives you instructions and the
code; it doesn't list what's coming in. The real activity is visible in the
dataset's **Overview** tab, and Meta warns it can take up to 30 minutes to show
it.

## The ad click

When a customer messages you from a **“Send WhatsApp message”** ad, their first
message usually carries hidden the ad they came from and a click identifier. We
store it alongside the contact without you having to do anything (you won't see
it on their profile: only the send to Meta uses it), and if the same customer
comes back through another ad, the latest one is kept.

With that there are two ways of telling Meta about the sale:

- **By phone number**, which is the usual one. Meta receives the fingerprint of
  the number and matches it to the person; if that person saw or tapped your
  ads, it attributes it with its usual rules. It works with a number connected
  by QR as long as the customer writes with a visible number and with a country:
  the ones that arrive with a hidden number are skipped.
- **By the exact click**, if you also save the **WhatsApp Business account ID**
  in the pixel. That ID belongs to numbers that are on the WhatsApp Business
  Platform (Meta's official API, the one for numbers registered through a
  provider or with Meta directly), and it's visible in **Business settings →
  Accounts → WhatsApp accounts**. If you connected by QR, you normally won't
  have it: leave it empty. With both pieces of information, the notification
  goes out marked as a messaging conversion and Meta hangs it off the specific
  ad.

The **Click attribution** badge on the card tells you the second route is
enabled; each notification uses it only if that customer arrived from an ad, and
if not, it goes by phone number.

## In Ads Manager

For purchases to appear in the campaign results, the ad has to read from the
same dataset. Edit the click-to-WhatsApp ad and scroll down to the **Tracking**
block, below the welcome template: tick **Website events** and check that the
dataset shown is the one you connected here. Click **Publish**.

  <img src="/images/pixel-de-meta/13-ads-seguimiento-antes.png" alt="Ad editor with the Tracking block and the Website events checkbox unticked" />
</Frame>

<Frame caption="Ticking “Website events” reveals the dataset with its ID. The “Your Meta pixel is not active” notice is normal: there is no website loading the pixel; the notifications arrive from the server.">

The real notifications appear in the dataset's **Overview** and in the campaign
reports later, with Meta's delay. If you've just connected, give it a few hours
before concluding that something is broken.

<Note>
Meta offers a purchase optimization for click-to-WhatsApp ads that is only
enabled once your account has shared a minimum number of purchases with it
through the Conversions API (Meta has published different figures depending on
the channel). When that objective appears on your account is decided by Meta,
not by this dashboard.
</Note>

## Common problems

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="“Generate access token” is greyed out">
    You're missing the permission on the business portfolio that owns the pixel
    (**Full control** or **Apps and integrations**). Ask whoever administers the
    portfolio for it, in **Business settings → Users → People**, or create a new
    dataset in a portfolio where you're already an admin (**Connect data →
    Web**).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="In “Connect data” I chose “Messaging” and it says no Page meets the requirements">
    That route is for numbers that are on the WhatsApp Business Platform. With a
    number connected by QR, Meta doesn't find an eligible Page. Go back and
    choose **Web**: the classic pixel receives this dashboard's notifications
    just the same.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="“Test connection” says Meta received it, but I don't see anything in “Test events”">
    That's expected: that Meta tab only gives instructions and the code, it
    doesn't list the notifications. The confirmation is in **Recent sends** (a
    **Sent** row) and, with up to 30 minutes of delay, in the dataset's
    **Overview**. If it doesn't show up there either after a few hours, check
    that the ID you pasted is the one for the dataset you're looking at.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Meta rejected the event: “Invalid OAuth access token”">
    The token belongs to another pixel, it was pasted incomplete or someone
    revoked it in Events Manager. Generate a new one, delete the pixel from the
    dashboard and create it with the good token. Write down the rules first:
    deleting erases them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A customer bought and there's no send at all">
    Check in this order: the pixel is **active** (green toggle); there's a sale
    rule **for that funnel** (each rule names its funnel); the sale was recorded
    by **your agent** in the conversation, you didn't enter it by hand; and the
    sale is less than 7 days old. If it all checks out, the send is in **Recent
    sends** with its status.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Almost everything comes out as “Skipped”">
    The reason is in each row. If it says the contact has no real phone number
    and no ad click, these are customers who wrote with their number hidden
    (WhatsApp lets you hide it; in your inbox you see them without a full phone
    number) or with no recognized country, and who didn't arrive from an ad:
    there is nothing Meta could match on and they are skipped on purpose. If it
    says the notification was too old, the system was down and recovered old
    occurrences: Meta doesn't accept anything more than 7 days old, so they are
    not sent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I want to change the token or the name">
    Today the dashboard doesn't let you edit them: delete the pixel and create
    it again with the new information. You lose its rules and its send history,
    not your contacts' click information.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I don't want to send anything yet">
    Create the pixel anyway and leave it with no rules, or switch it off with
    the toggle. In both cases no notification goes out, except the test one if
    you click **Test connection**.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What the pixel doesn't do

So you don't get a surprise:

- It doesn't send the sales you record by hand from **Customers**. Nor the sale
  the system records on its own when an order your agent didn't record in the
  chat is charged: for Meta only what the agent recorded exists.
- It doesn't subtract anything when a sale is voided.
- It doesn't send notifications more than 7 days old, nor while the pixel is
  paused or has no rules.
- It doesn't pass Meta the content of any conversation. What does travel: the
  fingerprints of the phone number, the name, the country and of an internal
  contact identifier; on sales, the amount, the currency, the product code and
  the order number; and, if you use click attribution, the click identifier and
  the one for your WhatsApp Business account, which are Meta's data, not yours.
- It doesn't move money and doesn't touch your campaigns or your ads: it only
  sends notifications to Meta.

## And now

Message your agent from another phone (with the number visible, and with the
test code already removed if you don't want it to come out as a test), let it
take you to your rule's chapter and look at **Recent sends**: that's the honest
test. If the ad reads from that dataset, in a few hours the real purchases start
appearing next to your ads.
